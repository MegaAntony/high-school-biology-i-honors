  • $\text{O}_2$ Origin Trap: The $\text{O}_2$ you breathe comes 100% from water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$) split at PSII, NOT from $\text{CO}_2$. $\text{CO}_2$ supplies carbon and oxygen for glucose.

Level 1 · Beginner

The big idea

☀️ Light → chemical energy

Photosynthesis captures light energy and stores part of it in the bonds of glucose. Plants, algae, and cyanobacteria are autotrophs: they make their own food.

A plant is a solar panel that stores energy as sugar instead of electricity. Respiration is what later unlocks that stored energy.

🔁 The two halves

Light reactions (thylakoid membrane): light + water → O₂ + ATP + NADPH.

Calvin cycle (stroma): CO₂ + ATP + NADPH → sugar (G3P → glucose).

The light reactions make the energy and the electrons. The Calvin cycle uses them to build the sugar.

The overall equation

6 CO2 + 6 H2O + light energy → C6H12O6 + 6 O2

carbon dioxide + water + light → glucose + oxygen

CO₂
Carbon source. Enters through stomata and is fixed into sugar.
H₂O
Split by light. Supplies electrons, H⁺, and the O₂ we breathe.
O₂
Byproduct of splitting water. Not from CO₂.

Key idea: photosynthesis is anabolic and endergonic. It builds big molecules and stores energy. Respiration is the reverse: catabolic and exergonic.

🌱 What plants need

  • Light, collected by leaves
  • CO₂, taken in through stomata (guard-cell pores)
  • Water, absorbed by roots and carried up the xylem
  • Minerals (N, P, Mg, and others) for building molecules

Leaves are wide and thin on purpose: they maximize light capture and gas exchange.

🫧 Stomata: the gate

Guard cells open and close each stoma. Open lets CO₂ in and O₂ and water vapor out. Closed saves water but blocks CO₂.

🌈 Light, wavelength, and color

Shorter wavelength = more energy per photon. Pigments absorb only certain wavelengths, so not all light can drive photosynthesis.

Level 2 · Core

Stage 1: Light-dependent reactions

Location: thylakoid membrane. Input: light, water, ADP + Pᵢ, NADP⁺. Output: O₂, ATP, NADPH.

Light hits PSII (P680)→ Water split → O₂ + H⁺ + e⁻→ ETC 1 pumps H⁺ into lumen→ ATP synthase → ATP→ Light hits PSI (P700)→ ETC 2 → NADP⁺ reductase→ NADPH

Step 1 · Photosystem II + ETC 1

  1. Pigments in PSII absorb light and pass energy to the reaction-center chlorophyll, P680.
  2. Two excited electrons leave P680 and enter ETC 1.
  3. P680 needs replacements, so the oxygen-evolving complex splits water: H₂O → ½ O₂ + 2 H⁺ + 2 e⁻.
  4. Energy from the falling electrons pumps H⁺ from the stroma into the thylakoid lumen.
  5. H⁺ flows back to the stroma through ATP synthase, which makes ATP (chemiosmosis).

⚠️ Trap: the O₂ comes from water, not CO₂. Also, H⁺ builds up inside the thylakoid.

Step 2 · Photosystem I + ETC 2

  1. Light excites electrons in P700 (PSI). They are re-energized.
  2. Electrons pass through ETC 2 to NADP⁺ reductase.
  3. The enzyme reduces NADP⁺ → NADPH (adds 2 e⁻ and H⁺).
  4. NADPH carries high-energy electrons and H⁺ into the stroma for the Calvin cycle.

⚠️ Trap: PSI does not split water. PSII does. PSI only re-energizes electrons that came from PSII.

Why PSII is "first" but has the higher number

The numbers reflect the order of discovery, not the order of electron flow. PSII acts first in the pathway, and PSI acts second.

Compare with respiration: both use an electron transport chain and H⁺ gradient to make ATP. The difference is the energy source: light here, food in respiration.

Level 2 · Core

Stage 2: Calvin cycle (light-independent)

Location: stroma. Input: CO₂, ATP, NADPH, RuBP. Output: G3P (→ glucose), ADP + Pᵢ, NADP⁺. "Light-independent" means it does not use light directly, but it depends on the light reactions' ATP and NADPH.

Calvin cycle overview (3 CO₂ + 9 ATP + 6 NADPH → 1 G3P). Image: "Calvin cycle overall" by Peter coxhead, CC0, via Wikimedia Commons.

1 · Carbon fixation

RuBisCO attaches CO₂ to the 5-carbon sugar RuBP, forming an unstable 6-carbon molecule that splits into two 3-PGA (3-carbon).

RuBisCO is the most abundant enzyme on Earth: it "makes life out of air."

2 · Reduction

ATP provides energy and NADPH provides electrons and H⁺, reducing 3-PGA into G3P. The output G3P leaves the cycle.

Each G3P is 3 carbons. Two G3P combine to make one 6-carbon glucose.

3 · Regeneration

Most G3P is recycled back into RuBP using ATP, so the cycle can continue. ADP, Pᵢ, and NADP⁺ return to the thylakoid to be reused.

It takes 6 turns (6 CO₂) to make 1 glucose.

Per-glucose accounting

Input (per glucose)Amount
CO₂6

Output1 glucose (from 2 G3P per turn, over 6 turns)

Matches the teacher's SA blueprint: 18 ATP and 12 NADPH per glucose.

Connecting the two stages

  • 🟢 Thylakoid → stroma: ATP and NADPH move out.
  • 🟢 Stroma → thylakoid: ADP, Pᵢ, and NADP⁺ come back.
  • 🔵 Light reactions store energy in ATP and NADPH. Calvin uses it to store energy in sugar.

If one half stops, the other backs up. That's the basis of the STEM case in the expert section.

Where the O₂ comes from (trap question)

Test writers love this one. The O₂ released in photosynthesis comes from water (photolysis at PSII), not from CO₂. CO₂ supplies the carbon in glucose.

Level 3 · Expert

Reason like the exam

Block one piece of the pathway and trace what piles up and what runs out. The Lab 4 STEM case is built on this.

1 · What if a stage stops? (teacher's STEM predictions) ▼
ConditionATPNADPHSugarWhy
PSII stops↓↓↓No water split, so no electrons and no H⁺ gradient. Nothing reaches PSI.
PSI stops=↓↓PSII and ETC 1 still make ATP, but electrons can't reach NADP⁺, so NADPH falls and the Calvin cycle stalls.
Calvin cycle stops↑↑↓Nothing uses ATP or NADPH, so they build up and the light reactions back up.

Expert rule: Find the blocked step, then look at what it produces (falls) and what feeds into it (backs up). PSII feeds everything, so blocking it drops all three. Blocking the Calvin cycle is the only case where ATP and NADPH rise.

2 · Hot, dry day: closed stomata ▼

Closing stomata saves water, but CO₂ can't get in. Two things happen:

  • Less CO₂ for RuBisCO, so the Calvin cycle slows. ATP and NADPH build up.
  • O₂ accumulates inside the leaf (it still forms from water). When O₂ is high and CO₂ is low, RuBisCO grabs O₂ instead, starting photorespiration, which wastes energy and carbon.

This is the teacher's Station 5 follow-up question. Full answer: CO₂ blocked → RuBisCO has no substrate → Calvin slows → sugar production falls.

3 · Coral bleaching (Lab 4 STEM case) ▼

Corals are animals, but they host photosynthetic algae (zooxanthellae) in a mutualism. The algae make glucose and O₂ for the coral; the coral gives the algae shelter and CO₂ and nitrogen.

When the algae lose their pigments or stop photosynthesis, the coral loses its food supply and turns white. The investigation asks which step the stressor blocks:

  • Stressor blocks PSII → ATP, NADPH, and sugar all fall (fastest way to starve the coral).
  • Stressor blocks PSI → NADPH and sugar fall, ATP holds.
  • Stressor blocks the Calvin cycle → sugar falls, ATP and NADPH pile up.
4 · Photosynthesis ↔ respiration (the cycle) ▼

The products of one process are the reactants of the other:

Photosynthesis: CO₂ + H₂O + light → glucose + O₂
Respiration: glucose + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O + ATP

Energy: photosynthesis stores light energy in glucose (anabolic, endergonic). Respiration releases that energy into ATP (catabolic, exergonic). Matter cycles; energy flows through once.

Plant cells do both. At night they respire, using stored glucose for ATP, because the light reactions have stopped.

5 · Extension (beyond the teacher's notes) ▼

Not required for the Oct 30 or Nov 2 exams.

  • C4 and CAM plants (corn, sugarcane, cacti) add CO₂ to a 4-carbon molecule before the Calvin cycle. That keeps CO₂ concentrated near RuBisCO, which reduces photorespiration in hot, dry conditions.
  • Action spectrum: plotting photosynthesis rate against wavelength matches chlorophyll's absorption spectrum, which is how scientists confirmed which pigments do the work.

Short-answer practice (Nov 2)

Write your answer on paper first, then open the model. Use Claim → Evidence → Reasoning and name the stage, location, and molecule each time.

Q1 · Trace one electron from water to NADPH. (4 pts)

Model: Light excites P680 in PSII. P680 has lost electrons, so water is split (photolysis), releasing O₂ and H⁺ and supplying replacement electrons. The excited electrons pass along ETC 1, pumping H⁺ into the thylakoid lumen. They then reach PSI, where light re-energizes them. Electrons pass through ETC 2 to NADP⁺ reductase, which reduces NADP⁺ to NADPH.

Scoring: water split at PSII (1) · electrons pass ETC 1 and H⁺ pumped into lumen (1) · PSI re-energizes electrons (1) · ETC 2 → NADP⁺ reductase → NADPH (1).

Q2 · Explain how the light reactions and the Calvin cycle depend on each other. (4 pts)

Model: The light reactions in the thylakoid make ATP and NADPH. These move into the stroma, where the Calvin cycle uses ATP for energy and NADPH for electrons and H⁺ to reduce 3-PGA into G3P. The Calvin cycle returns ADP, Pᵢ, and NADP⁺ to the thylakoid so the light reactions can run again.

Scoring: light reactions produce ATP/NADPH (1) · they move to the stroma (1) · Calvin uses ATP/NADPH to make G3P (1) · ADP/NADP⁺ return to thylakoid (1).

Q3 · A chemical blocks the Calvin cycle. Predict ATP, NADPH, and sugar, and explain. (3 pts)

Model: ATP and NADPH increase, because the light reactions keep running but nothing consumes them. Sugar production falls, because G3P is no longer made from CO₂.

Scoring: ATP increases (1) · NADPH increases (1) · sugar decreases with a reason (1).

Q4 · On a hot, dry day stomata close. Explain the effect on the Calvin cycle and on O₂ inside the leaf. (4 pts)

Model: Closed stomata block CO₂ from entering, so RuBisCO has less substrate and the Calvin cycle slows; G3P and sugar production drop. O₂ from water splitting cannot leave as easily and builds up in the leaf. When O₂ is high relative to CO₂, RuBisCO can bind O₂ instead of CO₂, which starts photorespiration and wastes energy and carbon.

Scoring: CO₂ can't enter (1) · Calvin cycle slows / less sugar (1) · O₂ builds up (1) · RuBisCO binds O₂ → photorespiration (1).

Q5 · Compare the role of O₂ in photosynthesis and in cellular respiration. (3 pts)

Model: In photosynthesis, O₂ is a byproduct of splitting water at PSII. In respiration, O₂ is a reactant: the final electron acceptor at the end of the ETC, combining with electrons and H⁺ to form water.

Scoring: photosynthesis O₂ is a byproduct of water splitting (1) · respiration O₂ is a reactant (1) · final electron acceptor in the ETC (1).
