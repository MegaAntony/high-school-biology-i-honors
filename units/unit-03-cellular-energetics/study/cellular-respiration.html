💡 High-Yield Exam Rules & Trap Alerts (Direct from Dr. Irwin's Keys)
  • Role of $\text{O}_2$: $\text{O}_2$ is a reactant that serves as the final electron acceptor at Complex IV, combining with electrons and $\text{H}^+$ to form $\text{H}_2\text{O}$. Without $\text{O}_2$, the entire ETC halts.
  • Proton Gradient Orientation: $\text{H}^+$ is pumped from the matrix OUT into the intermembrane space (IMS). IMS is acidic (low pH); matrix is alkaline (high pH).
  • Substrate vs. Oxidative Phosphorylation: Glycolysis and Krebs use substrate-level phosphorylation (direct enzyme transfer, 4 ATP total). The ETC uses oxidative phosphorylation (chemiosmosis via ATP synthase, 32–34 ATP).
  • Glycolysis Universality: Occurs in the cytosol, requires NO oxygen, and is shared by all living organisms (evolutionary evidence).
  • Cyanide vs. Oligomycin: Cyanide stops $\text{H}^+$ pumping ($\text{H}^+$ gradient disappears, NADH high). Oligomycin only blocks ATP synthase ($\text{H}^+$ gradient gets stuck at maximum high in IMS).

Level 1 · Beginner

The big idea

🍬 What is it?

Cellular respiration is how cells break apart glucose (sugar) and capture some of its energy in a form the cell can spend: ATP.

Think of glucose as a full charged battery. Respiration slowly drains it in small steps, storing the energy in ATP "coins" instead of letting it all out as heat at once.

💰 ATP = cell money

ATP (adenosine triphosphate) powers muscle movement, building molecules, and sending nerve signals. When ATP loses a phosphate it becomes ADP and releases energy.

Cells spend ATP all day. Respiration is how they earn it back.

The overall equation

C6H12O6 + 6 O2 → 6 CO2 + 6 H2O + energy (ATP)

glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + ATP

Glucose
Fuel. Gets oxidized (loses electrons).
Oxygen
Final electron acceptor. Gets reduced.
CO₂ and H₂O
Waste products. CO₂ is exhaled.

Why does oxygen appear? Oxygen is needed at the very end, to accept the electrons. If it isn't there, the whole energy-making line jams (see Expert, section 4).

⚡ Oxidation & reduction

OIL RIG: Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain (of electrons).

Glucose loses electrons (oxidized). Those electrons ride on carrier molecules NAD⁺ → NADH and FAD → FADH₂, then reach oxygen (reduced to water).

🫁 Aerobic vs. anaerobic

Aerobic (with O₂): full pathway, 36–38 ATP per glucose.

Anaerobic (no O₂): only glycolysis plus fermentation, 2 ATP per glucose.

Your body does both. During hard sprints muscles can't get enough O₂ fast enough, so they lean on fermentation.

Level 2 · Core

The four stages, in order

Follow the carbon, the electrons, and the energy through the pathway. Each card gives location, inputs, outputs, and what to remember for the test.

Glucose→ 1 Glycolysis→ 2 Pyruvate oxidation→ 3 Krebs cycle→ 4 ETC + chemiosmosis→ ATP + H₂O

1 · Glycolysis

Where: Cytosol (no membrane-bound organelles, no O₂ needed).

In: 1 glucose, 2 ATP (invested), 2 NAD⁺, ADP + Pᵢ

Out: 2 pyruvate (3C each), 2 NADH, 4 ATP made → net 2 ATP

  1. Energy investment: 2 ATP activate glucose.
  2. The 6-carbon sugar splits into two 3-carbon molecules.
  3. Oxidation: electrons go to NAD⁺ → NADH.
  4. Substrate-level phosphorylation makes ATP directly.

⚠️ Trap: glycolysis makes 4 ATP but spends 2, so the net is 2.

2 · Pyruvate oxidation (link reaction)

Where: Mitochondrial matrix.

In: 2 pyruvate, 2 NAD⁺, 2 coenzyme A

Out: 2 acetyl-CoA, 2 NADH, 2 CO₂, 0 ATP

  1. One carbon leaves each pyruvate as CO₂.
  2. Electrons reduce NAD⁺ → NADH.
  3. CoA attaches to the 2-carbon acetyl group.

⚠️ Trap: no ATP is made here. Students often count it as a stage that makes energy.

3 · Krebs cycle (citric acid cycle)

Where: Mitochondrial matrix.

In: 2 acetyl-CoA, NAD⁺, FAD, ADP + Pᵢ (per glucose: the cycle turns twice)

Out (per glucose): 4 CO₂, 6 NADH, 2 FADH₂, 2 ATP

  1. Acetyl group joins 4-carbon oxaloacetate → 6-carbon citrate.
  2. Citrate is oxidized and decarboxylated (CO₂ released).
  3. NAD⁺ and FAD pick up electrons.
  4. Oxaloacetate is regenerated so the cycle continues.

⚠️ Trap: the cycle makes very little ATP directly. Its real job is loading NADH and FADH₂ for the ETC.

4 · Electron transport chain + chemiosmosis

Where: Inner mitochondrial membrane (the cristae).

In: 10 NADH, 2 FADH₂, O₂, ADP + Pᵢ

Out: about 32–34 ATP, H₂O, and regenerated NAD⁺ and FAD

  1. NADH and FADH₂ drop their electrons into the chain.
  2. Energy from the electrons pumps H⁺ from the matrix into the intermembrane space.
  3. H⁺ builds a gradient ("water behind a dam").
  4. H⁺ flows back through ATP synthase, which makes ATP (chemiosmosis).
  5. O₂ accepts the electrons and H⁺ to form H₂O.

⚠️ Trap: O₂ is not used until the very end. Its job is the final electron acceptor.

Compartment Guide

Where Each Stage Takes Place

Four distinct subcellular zones work in sequential harmony to convert 1 glucose into 36–38 ATP:

  • 🟠
    Cytosol (Cytoplasm): Glycolysis
    The only stage outside mitochondria; requires no O₂ and no organelles. Net 2 ATP + 2 NADH.
  • 🔵
    Mitochondrial Matrix: Pyruvate Oxidation & Krebs Cycle
    Pyruvate converts to Acetyl-CoA (2 CO₂, 2 NADH), then 2 turns of Krebs release 4 CO₂, 2 ATP, 6 NADH, 2 FADH₂.
  • 🟣
    Inner Membrane (Cristae): ETC & ATP Synthase
    10 NADH + 2 FADH₂ drop electrons into Complexes I–IV. Energy pumps H⁺ out into intermembrane space.
  • ⚪
    Intermembrane Space: H⁺ Reservoir (Proton Gradient)
    High H⁺ concentration rushes back into matrix through ATP Synthase (Chemiosmosis) making 32–34 ATP.

Bars use the 36–38 total as the whole. Oxidative phosphorylation makes about 90% of the ATP.

Step-by-step worked example

  1. Glycolysis: 4 ATP made − 2 invested = 2 net.
  2. Pyruvate oxidation: 2 pyruvate → 2 NADH, 2 CO₂, 0 ATP.
  3. Krebs (2 turns): 2 ATP, 6 NADH, 2 FADH₂, 4 CO₂.
  4. Count the carriers: NADH = 2 + 2 + 6 = 10; FADH₂ = 2.
  5. ETC: 10 NADH + 2 FADH₂ → 32–34 ATP.
  6. Total: 2 + 2 + 32–34 = 36–38 ATP.

Note: the course uses 36–38 ATP. Your teacher explains that ATP totals vary by cell and by convention, so use the numbers from the unit when you write answers.

CO₂ and O₂ counting (common test items)

• CO₂ out: 2 from pyruvate oxidation + 4 from Krebs = 6 per glucose.

• O₂ in: 6 per glucose, all used at the end of the ETC (as the final electron acceptor).

• H₂O out: 6 per glucose, formed when O₂ accepts electrons and H⁺ at the end of the ETC.

Level 3 · Expert

Reason like the exam

The exam asks you to predict what happens when one step breaks. Trace the carriers, the protons, and the ATP.

1 · Blocked steps: the poison case study ▼

Lab 3 (Poison Case Study) compares each victim with a healthy control. Use the pattern below and you can answer any blocked-step question.

Blocked stepPyruvateMito NADHIntermembrane H⁺Why
Glycolysis (2-deoxyglucose)↓↓↓No pyruvate to feed the rest of the pathway.
Pyruvate dehydrogenase (arsenic)↑↓↓Pyruvate backs up; no NADH or acetyl-CoA made for Krebs.
ETC Complex IV (cyanide)=↑↓Electrons can't reach O₂, so NADH backs up and H⁺ pumping stops.
ATP synthase (oligomycin)==↑↑The chain still pumps H⁺, but the H⁺ can't flow back, so it piles up and ATP stops.

Expert rule: Ask "what is upstream and downstream?" Anything upstream of the block piles up. Anything downstream starves.

Cyanide blocks the end, so NADH, the upstream carrier, rises. Oligomycin blocks the H⁺ exit, so the gradient rises. Both stop ATP.

2 · Mitochondrial disease (Kearns-Sayre) ▼

If oxidative phosphorylation is damaged, the ETC cannot pump H⁺ or run ATP synthase. Two things follow:

  • ATP falls from 36–38 to roughly 2–4 per glucose (teacher's key).
  • NAD⁺ and FAD run out, because NADH and FADH₂ can't drop their electrons. Pyruvate oxidation and Krebs then stop.

Cells that need the most ATP, such as muscle and the brain, suffer most. They fall back on glycolysis and lactic acid fermentation.

3 · Without oxygen: fermentation ▼
TypeWhoProductATP per glucose
Lactic acid fermentationAnimal cells (muscle), some bacteriaLactate (lactic acid)2 (glycolysis only)
Alcoholic fermentationYeast and some microbesEthanol + CO₂2 (glycolysis only)
Aerobic respirationMost eukaryotes with O₂CO₂ + H₂O36–38

Why fermentation exists: it converts pyruvate and NADH back into NAD⁺. Glycolysis needs NAD⁺ to keep making ATP. Fermentation adds no new ATP; it just keeps the 2 ATP from glycolysis running.

4 · Why oxygen is "the final electron acceptor" ▼

The ETC is a conveyor belt. Electrons move down it, losing energy each step. Something has to take the electrons off the end, or the belt stops.

Oxygen is that something. It is pulled strongly toward electrons, so it takes them and combines them with H⁺ to form water.

Chain of logic for a 3-mark answer: No O₂ → electrons can't leave the chain → NADH and FADH₂ stay loaded → NAD⁺ and FAD aren't regenerated → pyruvate oxidation and Krebs stop → H⁺ gradient collapses → ATP synthase stops.

5 · Extension (beyond the teacher's notes) ▼

Not required for the Oct 30 or Nov 2 exams. Read it to see where the topic goes next.

  • Regulation: Glycolysis is controlled at phosphofructokinase. High ATP slows it down, so the cell doesn't burn glucose when energy is plentiful.
  • Why some cells prefer glycolysis: Fast-growing cancer cells and some fast-twitch muscles make lots of lactate even with oxygen around. The reason is speed, not oxygen shortage.
  • Brown fat: uncoupling proteins let H⁺ leak back into the matrix without ATP synthase, so energy becomes heat.

Exam practice · Short answer

Short-answer practice (Nov 2)

Write your answer on paper first, then open the model. Use Claim → Evidence → Reasoning (CER) and name the stage, location, and molecule each time.

Q1 · Trace the carbon from one glucose molecule through respiration. Where does each carbon go? (4 pts)

Model: One glucose (6 C) splits in glycolysis into two pyruvate (3 C each), which stay in the cell. In pyruvate oxidation, each pyruvate loses one carbon as CO₂ (2 CO₂ total) and becomes acetyl-CoA (2 C). In the Krebs cycle, the acetyl groups are fully oxidized, releasing 4 CO₂. So all 6 carbons leave as CO₂: 2 from pyruvate oxidation and 4 from Krebs.

Scoring: glucose → pyruvate (1) · CO₂ from pyruvate oxidation (1) · 4 CO₂ from Krebs (1) · total 6 C leave as CO₂ (1).

Q2 · Explain how the ETC produces ATP, and name the molecule that makes it. (4 pts)

Model: NADH and FADH₂ donate electrons to the ETC on the inner mitochondrial membrane. As electrons pass along the chain, energy pumps H⁺ from the matrix into the intermembrane space, creating an electrochemical gradient. H⁺ flows back through ATP synthase, and the flow drives ADP + Pᵢ → ATP. This is chemiosmosis, and it is oxidative phosphorylation. O₂ accepts the electrons at the end and forms H₂O.

Scoring: electron carriers donate electrons (1) · H⁺ pumped into intermembrane space (1) · H⁺ flows through ATP synthase, making ATP (1) · O₂ is the final electron acceptor forming H₂O (1).

Q3 · A poison blocks ATP synthase (oligomycin). Predict the change in intermembrane H⁺ and ATP, and explain. (4 pts)

Model: The ETC keeps pumping H⁺ into the intermembrane space, but ATP synthase is blocked, so H⁺ cannot flow back into the matrix. Intermembrane H⁺ increases sharply. Without the H⁺ flow, ATP synthase cannot make ATP, so ATP production falls to the substrate-level amount (about 4 ATP). NADH stays about the same because the chain still runs.

Scoring: H⁺ increases (1) · H⁺ can't flow through ATP synthase (1) · ATP falls (1) · explanation links the blocked channel to the drop (1).

Q4 · Explain why an animal cell with no oxygen produces lactic acid and only 2 ATP. (3 pts)

Model: Without O₂, the ETC cannot run, so NADH cannot give up its electrons and NAD⁺ is not regenerated. Pyruvate cannot enter the Krebs cycle. Lactic acid fermentation reduces pyruvate to lactate, which regenerates NAD⁺ so glycolysis can keep running. Glycolysis is the only ATP source, giving a net of 2 ATP.

Scoring: no O₂ means the ETC stops and NAD⁺ isn't regenerated (1) · lactate regenerates NAD⁺ (1) · glycolysis gives only 2 net ATP (1).

Q5 · Kearns-Sayre syndrome damages oxidative phosphorylation. Explain the effect on ATP and on the Krebs cycle. (4 pts)

Model: Oxidative phosphorylation supplies 32–34 of the 36–38 ATP, so ATP falls to only the substrate-level amount (about 2–4 per glucose). Because NADH and FADH₂ can't pass electrons to the ETC, NAD⁺ and FAD are not regenerated. Pyruvate oxidation and the Krebs cycle then stop, so the cell depends mainly on glycolysis and fermentation.

Scoring: ATP drops to about 2–4 (1) · ETC/ATP synthase can't make most ATP (1) · NAD⁺/FAD not regenerated (1) · Krebs/pyruvate oxidation stop (1).
