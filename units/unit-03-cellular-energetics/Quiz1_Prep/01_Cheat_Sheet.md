# Cheat Sheet

## Enzymes
- Protein catalysts; **lower Ea**, do NOT change ΔG, not consumed. Say "denatured", never "killed".
- Lock & key (rigid) vs **induced fit** (active site adjusts).
- Temp: rate rises to optimum, then drops sharply (denatured). Cold slows, does NOT denature.
- pH: each enzyme has an optimum (pepsin ~2, most ~7, trypsin ~8). Extremes denature.
- Substrate ↑ → rate rises then **plateaus** (enzymes saturated). More enzyme → higher Vmax.
- **Competitive inhibitor**: binds active site; overcome by more substrate; same Vmax.
- **Noncompetitive/allosteric**: binds elsewhere, changes shape; NOT overcome; lower Vmax.

## ATP
- Adenine + ribose + 3 phosphates. ATP → ADP + Pi (hydrolysis, releases energy, exergonic).
- ADP + Pi → ATP (dehydration synthesis, endergonic). Coupling: exergonic drives endergonic.

## Cellular respiration: C6H12O6 + 6O2 → 6CO2 + 6H2O + ATP
| Stage | Where | In → Out |
|---|---|---|
| Glycolysis | cytoplasm | glucose → 2 pyruvate, 2 ATP net, 2 NADH |
| Pyruvate oxidation | matrix | pyruvate → acetyl-CoA, CO2, NADH |
| Krebs | matrix | CO2, NADH, FADH2, ATP |
| ETC + chemiosmosis | inner membrane (cristae) | NADH/FADH2 → ~26-28 ATP, O2 → H2O |
- O2 = final electron acceptor. ATP synthase makes ATP as H+ flows down gradient into matrix.
- Fermentation (no O2): regenerates **NAD+** so glycolysis continues; no extra ATP. Lactic acid (muscle) / ethanol + CO2 (yeast).
- Cristae = more surface area for ETC.

## Poison case study
| Poison | Blocks | Pyruvate | Intermembrane H+ | NADH |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2-deoxyglucose | start of glycolysis | LOW | low | low |
| Arsenic | pyruvate → Krebs | HIGH | low | low |
| Oligomycin | ATP synthase | — | VERY HIGH | — |
| Cyanide | ETC (complex IV) | — | low | VERY HIGH |
(Normal: pyruvate 0.09-0.15, H+ 0.25-0.35, NADH 0.25-0.40 mM.)

## Photosynthesis (intro)
6CO2 + 6H2O + light → C6H12O6 + 6O2. Chloroplast. Light reactions (thylakoid) make ATP + NADPH; Calvin cycle (stroma) makes sugar.
Photosynthesis and respiration are reverse-looking equations, products of one are reactants of the other.
