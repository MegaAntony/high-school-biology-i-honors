## 📝 Item Transcriptions & Verified Rationales

### Item 1 (ID: `HbioU3Q1 Item 1`)
- **Question**: The illustration shows an enzyme and a molecule labeled `E` fitting directly into the active site, physically blocking the normal substrate. *The illustration below represents what process?*
- **A. Competitive inhibition**
- **B. Non-competitive inhibition**
- **C. Allosteric Activation**
- **D. Denaturation**
- **Key**: **A. Competitive inhibition**
- *Rationale*: Competitive inhibitors bind directly into the enzyme's active site, competing with the substrate.

### Item 2 (ID: `HbioU3Q1 Item 10`)
- **Question**: *Which statement below accurately represents the difference between aerobic respiration and fermentation?*
- **A. Much less ATP is generated during fermentation than during aerobic respiration**
- **B. $CO_2$ is only produced during fermentation**
- **C. Glucose is split into 2 molecules of pyruvate only during aerobic respiration**
- **D. Oxygen is required for both processes**
- **Key**: **A. Much less ATP is generated during fermentation than during aerobic respiration**
- *Rationale*: Fermentation yields only $2\text{ net ATP}$ (from glycolysis), whereas aerobic respiration yields $36\text{–}38\text{ ATP}$ per glucose.

### Item 3 (ID: `HbioU3Q1 Item 11`)
- **Question**: Energy diagram showing high starting reactant energy dropping to low product energy ($\Delta G < 0$). *Consider the energy diagram for a chemical reaction in the figure below and pick the terms that best fill in the blanks: In the reaction energy is being _________ because this demonstrates a __________ reaction.*
- **A. released; anabolic**
- **B. released; catabolic**
- **C. absorbed; anabolic**
- **D. absorbed; catabolic**
- **Key**: **B. released; catabolic**
- *Rationale*: Reactions with a net negative free energy ($\Delta G < 0$) are exergonic/catabolic, releasing energy.

### Item 4 (ID: `HbioU3Q1 Item 12`)
- **Question**: *Which of the following processes is used by all living organisms?*
- **A. Glycolysis**
- **B. Oxidative phosphorylation**
- **C. Lactic acid fermentation**
- **D. Alcohol Fermentation**
- **Key**: **A. Glycolysis**
- *Rationale*: Glycolysis takes place in the cytoplasm, requires no membrane-bound organelles or oxygen, and evolved in the most primitive cells on anoxic early Earth.

### Item 7 (ID: `HbioU3Q1 Item 15`)
- **Question**: *If you have been inactive for a long period of time and you suddenly start to exercise at a high intensity, your muscles will likely become fatigued (tired) quickly. Which of the following statements BEST describes why this is the case?*
- **A. Your body has too much oxygen and the rate of cellular respiration is too great, causing overproduction of ATP.**
- **B. Your body has too much oxygen and the rate of cellular respiration is too low, causing pyruvate to build up.**
- **C. Your body is not getting enough oxygen and is going through lactic acid fermentation, causing lactate buildup and lack of ATP.**
- **D. Your body is not getting enough oxygen and is going through alcoholic fermentation, causing carbon dioxide to build up.**
- **Key**: **C**
- *Rationale*: Muscle cells switch to lactic acid fermentation when oxygen delivery cannot keep up with energetic demand.

### Item 8 (ID: `HbioU3Q1 Item 16`)
- **Question**: *Which of the following best describes the purpose of fermentation?*
- **A. To convert pyruvate to acetyl CoA so it can enter the Krebs cycle in the presence of oxygen.**
- **B. To make more NADH to be used in the ETC in the presence of oxygen.**
- **C. To make more NADH to be used in glycolysis to make more ADP and NAD+ in the absence of oxygen.**
- **D. To make more NAD+ to be reused in glycolysis to make more ATP and NADH in the absence of oxygen.**
- **Key**: **D**
- *Rationale*: Fermentation oxidizes $\text{NADH} \to \text{NAD}^+$ so glycolysis has electron acceptors to continue producing $2\text{ ATP}$.

### Item 9 (ID: `HbioU3Q1 Item 17`)
- **Question**: *A scientist sets up 4 tubes for an experiment. Tube 1 contains only water. Tube 2 contains a live snail. Tube 3 contains a live aquatic plant. Tube 4 both a live aquatic plant and a live snail. Which tubes contain at least one organism carrying out cellular respiration?*
- **A. Tubes 2 and 3 only**
- **B. Tubes 3 and 4 only**
- **C. Tubes 2, 3, and 4 only**
- **D. Tubes 2 and 4 only**
- **Key**: **C**
- *Rationale*: Both plants (autotrophs) and animals (heterotrophs) carry out cellular respiration 24 hours a day in mitochondria.

### Item 11 (ID: `HbioU3Q1 Item 19`)
- **Question**: *Which of the following statements BEST describes how ATP is renewed in the body?*
- **A. ATP is regenerated when the energy from a catabolic reaction in the cell powers the phosphorylation of a molecule of ADP.**
- **B. ATP is regenerated when the energy from an anabolic reaction in the cell powers the phosphorylation of a molecule of ADP.**
- **C. ATP is renewed through the process of photosynthesis, where sunlight energy is converted into chemical energy.**
- **D. ATP is constantly renewed through the consumption of high-energy foods and drinks, providing an immediate energy boost.**
- **Key**: **A**
- *Rationale*: Energy released by exergonic catabolic reactions (like glucose breakdown) is coupled to phosphorylate $\text{ADP} + \text{P}_i \to \text{ATP}$.

### Item 12 (ID: `HbioU3Q1 Item 2`)
- **Question**: *How does an enzyme catalyze a biological reaction?*
- **A. It increases the net energy difference between reactants and products.**
- **B. It increases the kinetic energy of reactants, thereby increasing their tendency to collide.**
- **C. It decreases the kinetic energy of reactants, enabling them to undergo chemical change more easily.**
- **D. It decreases the activation energy necessary to initiate (start) the chemical change.**
- **Key**: **D**
- *Rationale*: Enzymes lower the activation energy barrier by stabilizing the transition state via induced fit.

### Item 13 (ID: `HbioU3Q1 Item 20`)
- **Question**: *Which of the following best describes the importance of the krebs cycle?*
- **A. To split glucose in order to be processed by the mitochondria.**
- **B. To create ATP in the absence of oxygen.**
- **C. To create a gradient of protons (H+) to flow through ATP synthase.**
- **D. To generate many electron carriers to be used in the ETC.**
- **Key**: **D**
- *Rationale*: The Krebs cycle's primary contribution is harvesting high-energy electrons, generating $6\text{ NADH}$ and $2\text{ FADH}_2$ per glucose for the ETC.

### Item 14 (ID: `HbioU3Q1 Item 21`)
- **Question**: *What molecule is created as a byproduct of oxidative phosphorylation?*
- **A. Carbon Dioxide**
- **B. Water**
- **C. Lactic Acid**
- **D. NADH**
- **Key**: **B. Water**
- *Rationale*: Oxygen serves as the terminal electron acceptor, bonding with protons ($2\text{H}^+$) and electrons to form metabolic water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$).

### Item 15 (ID: `HbioU3Q1 Item 22`)
- **Question**: *All of the following are true about enzymes EXCEPT:*
- **A. All living cells have enzymes that can be reused in reactions.**
- **B. Enzymes are biological catalysts that increase the rate of chemical reactions.**
- **C. Enzymes are consumed (used up) in the reactions that they catalyze.**
- **D. Enzymes are highly specific in their substrate binding.**
- **Key**: **C**
- *Rationale*: Enzymes are catalysts and emerge from chemical reactions completely unchanged and ready to bind another substrate.

### Item 16 (ID: `HbioU3Q1 Item 3`)
- **Question**: *Carbonic anhydrase assists in the rapid conversion of carbon dioxide and water into carbonic acid, protons, and bicarbonate ions. If phenol is added to this chemical reaction, the amount of product produced is decreased because phenol binds to the active site of carbonic anhydrase, decreasing the likelihood that carbon dioxide will bind. What is the role of phenol?*
- **A. Enzyme**
- **B. Substrate**
- **C. Competitive inhibitor**
- **D. Noncompetitive inhibitor**
- **Key**: **C. Competitive inhibitor**
- *Rationale*: Because phenol binds to the active site, it competes directly with carbon dioxide.

### Item 17 (ID: `HbioU3Q1 Item 4`)
- **Question**: *A student set up four test tubes containing a starch solution for an experiment to model digestion... In which test tube would starch digestion occur MOST quickly?*
- **A. Tube 1: No amylase, room temperature (25°C)**
- **B. Tube 2: No amylase, body temperature (37°C)**
- **C. Tube 3: Amylase present, room temperature (25°C)**
- **D. Tube 4: Amylase present, body temperature (37°C)**
- **Key**: **D**
- *Rationale*: Amylase requires its physiological enzyme catalyst and performs fastest at human body temperature ($37^\circ\text{C}$).

### Item 18 (ID: `HbioU3Q1 Item 5`)
- **Question**: *The graph below shows the reactivity of lactase at different temperatures. At which temperature would lactase be considered denatured?*
- **A. 50 degrees C**
- **B. 35 degrees C**
- **C. 40 degrees C**
- **D. 30 degrees C**
- **Key**: **A. 50 degrees C**
- *Rationale*: The lactase curve peaks near $37^\circ\text{C}$ and drops steeply toward zero at $50^\circ\text{C}\text{–}60^\circ\text{C}$ as heat breaks tertiary bonds.
