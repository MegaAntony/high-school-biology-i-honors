🔄 1. Master Comparison: Cellular Respiration vs. Photosynthesis (Station 3, 4, 5 & 6)
⚡ Core Cycling Principle: Matter ($\text{C}, \text{H}, \text{O}$) cycles continuously between chloroplasts and mitochondria, whereas energy flows one-way: $\text{Sunlight} \xrightarrow{\text{Photosynthesis}} \text{Glucose (Chemical Bonds)} \xrightarrow{\text{Respiration}} \text{ATP} \longrightarrow \text{Heat}$.
$$\textbf{Photosynthesis (Anabolic / Endergonic, }\Delta G > 0\textbf{):}\quad 6\text{CO}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{Light Energy} \xrightarrow{\text{Chloroplast}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\text{O}_2$$
$$\textbf{Cellular Respiration (Catabolic / Exergonic, }\Delta G < 0\textbf{):}\quad \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\text{O}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Mitochondria}} 6\text{CO}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O} + 36\text{–}38\text{ ATP}$$
Feature / Question Cellular Respiration (Unit 3B) Photosynthesis (Unit 3C)
WHO performs it? ALL Eukaryotes (Plants/Autotrophs AND Animals/Fungi/Heterotrophs!) Photoautotrophs (Producers) only: Plants, algae, cyanobacteria
WHERE does it occur? Cytosol (Glycolysis) & Mitochondria (Matrix = Link & Krebs; Cristae = ETC) Chloroplast (Thylakoid Membrane = Light Rxns; Stroma = Calvin Cycle)
Electron Carriers Used $\text{NAD}^+ \to \text{NADH}$ ($10$) and $\text{FAD} \to \text{FADH}_2$ ($2$) $\text{NADP}^+ \to \text{NADPH}$ (P = Photosynthesis!)
ETC Location & $\text{H}^+$ Pumping Inner Mitochondrial Membrane (Cristae): $\text{H}^+$ pumped from Matrix $\to$ Intermembrane Space; diffuses back into Matrix via ATP Synthase. Thylakoid Membrane: $\text{H}^+$ pumped from Stroma $\to$ Thylakoid Lumen (Interior); diffuses back into Stroma via ATP Synthase.
Source of Electrons for ETC Oxidation of Glucose ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 \to \text{CO}_2$) delivered by $\text{NADH}/\text{FADH}_2$. Photolysis (splitting) of Water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$) at Photosystem II ($\text{H}_2\text{O} \to \frac{1}{2}\text{O}_2 + 2\text{H}^+ + 2\text{e}^-$).
Final Electron Acceptor at End of ETC Oxygen ($\text{O}_2$) $\longrightarrow$ combines with $\text{e}^-$ and $\text{H}^+$ to form $\text{H}_2\text{O}$. $\text{NADP}^+$ (at Photosystem I via $\text{NADP}^+$ reductase) $\longrightarrow$ forms $\text{NADPH}$.
Role of ATP Main final energy output ($36\text{–}38\text{ ATP}$) used for all cellular work. Produced in Light Reactions (ETC 1) and immediately consumed in the Calvin Cycle ($18\text{ ATP}$ per glucose) to fix $\text{CO}_2$ into sugar.

Station 1 & Station 2 Key: Enzymes, Activation Energy & Inhibition Graphs Review Handout p. 1–2
  • Catabolic vs. Anabolic Enzyme Diagram: Catabolic because one bonded two-hexagon substrate is cleaved into two separate product molecules.
  • Competitive vs. Non-Competitive Inhibition:
    • Competitive (Left image): Mimics substrate shape and binds directly inside the active site. Can be overcome by adding excess substrate ($V_{\max}$ unchanged).
    • Non-Competitive / Allosteric (Right image): Binds to an allosteric site, warping the 3D conformation of the active site so substrate cannot fit. Cannot be overcome by adding substrate ($V_{\max}$ lowered).
  • Graph A (pH): Acidic optimum = Pepsin ($\text{pH } 2$); Neutral = Salivary Amylase ($\text{pH } 7$); Alkaline = Trypsin ($\text{pH } 8$). Salivary amylase at $\text{pH } 2$ has 0 activity because extreme acid disrupts ionic/hydrogen bonds and denatures its active site.
  • Graph B (Temperature): Optimum = $\sim 37^\circ\text{C}\text{–}40^\circ\text{C}$ (Point B). Between B and C, high heat denatures the enzyme (unfolds the active site so substrate cannot bind).
  • Graph C (Substrate Saturation): From $a \to b$, more substrate increases collision frequency and rate. At $c$, saturation ($V_{\max}$) occurs because 100% of active sites are occupied.
  • Station 2 Energy Graphs: Graph A ($\Delta G < 0$) = Exergonic (energy released, products lower than reactants, e.g., Cellular Respiration). Graph B ($\Delta G > 0$) = Endergonic (energy added, products higher than reactants, e.g., Photosynthesis). Enzymes speed up reactions solely by lowering Activation Energy ($E_a$) without changing $\Delta G$.

Station 7: Cellular Respiration (1–10)

  1. C — Glycolysis (requires no organelles and no oxygen; evolved first on anoxic early Earth).
  2. B — Anaerobic respiration (fermentation produces alcohol or lactic acid).
  3. C — Aerobic respiration because it produces 36 ATP (vs. 2 ATP in anaerobic).
  4. A — Electron transport chain (produces 32–34 ATP via oxidative phosphorylation).
  5. C — Fermentation (regenerates $\text{NAD}^+$ when $\text{O}_2$ is absent).
  6. D ($\text{O}_2; \text{H}_2\text{O}$) — $\text{H}^+$ is pumped into intermembrane space, $\text{O}_2$ is reduced to form $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ byproduct.
  7. B (II, IV, and V) — II is backwards ($\text{FAD}$ is reduced to $\text{FADH}_2$), IV is Pyruvate Oxidation, and V lists products as reactants.
  8. A — Cell A is an animal cell in an anaerobic environment (lactic acid + 2 ATP); Cell B is an animal cell in an aerobic environment ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$ + up to 32–38 ATP).
  9. C — Water; ATP (heterotrophs convert food mass + $\text{O}_2$ into $\text{CO}_2$, $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, and $\text{ATP}$).
  10. C — Oxygen (consumed by germinating peas during aerobic respiration).

Station 8: Photosynthesis (1–10)

  1. D — Converting oxygen and hydrogen into water (that happens in the mitochondrial ETC, not photosynthesis!).
  2. B — Chlorophyll (absorbs red and blue-violet light, reflects green).
  3. D — ATP (made in light reactions along with NADPH to power the Calvin cycle).
  4. C — Stomata (leaf pores regulated by guard cells for $\text{CO}_2/\text{O}_2$ gas exchange).
  5. C — Oxygen concentration (light, temp, and $\text{CO}_2$ directly affect photosynthesis rate; $\text{O}_2$ is a product).
  6. C — Green (least absorbed / mostly reflected by chlorophyll, so slowest $\text{O}_2$ release).
  7. C — thylakoid; light-dependent.
  8. C — PS I involves $\text{NADP}^+$ Reductase (making NADPH) while PS II involves ATP Synthase (via ETC 1 $\text{H}^+$ gradient) and splits water.
  9. A (I, V, and VI) — Light-independent reaction (I), occurs in stroma (V), glucose made from $\text{CO}_2$ (VI).
  10. B — Glucose and oxygen gas (products) have higher energy levels compared to water and carbon dioxide (reactants).

  • Bicarbonate Indicator Rule (Page 16 Verbatim Scale):
    • Darkness (Foil-Covered Vial): Photosynthesis = OFF, Cellular Respiration = ON $\longrightarrow$ Net $\text{CO}_2$ added $\longrightarrow$ turns from red ($\text{pH } 8.4$) through orange ($\text{pH } 8.0$) to yellow ($\text{pH } 7.6$).
    • Atmospheric Control ($0.04\%\text{ CO}_2$): Equilibrium $\text{CO}_2$ $\longrightarrow$ $\text{pH } 8.4$ (red).
    • Bright Light (Uncovered Vial): Photosynthesis Rate $>$ Respiration Rate $\longrightarrow$ Net $\text{CO}_2$ removed $\longrightarrow$ turns from red ($\text{pH } 8.4$) through magenta ($\text{pH } 8.8$) to dark purple ($\text{pH } 9.2$).
  • Model CER (Page 17):
    • Claim: Exposure to light drives net photosynthesis in algae beads, consuming dissolved $\text{CO}_2$ and raising the solution pH, whereas darkness halts photosynthesis while cellular respiration continues, releasing $\text{CO}_2$ and lowering pH.
    • Evidence: Both vials started at red ($\text{pH } 8.4$). After incubation, the uncovered vial in the light turned purple ($\text{pH } 9.2$), while the aluminum-foil-covered dark vial turned yellow ($\text{pH } 7.6$).
    • Reasoning: In the light vial, chlorophyll absorbed photons to produce ATP and NADPH in the light reactions, enabling the Calvin cycle to fix dissolved $\text{CO}_2$ into glucose faster than respiration released it, reducing acidity and raising pH to $9.2$. In the foil-covered vial, lack of light halted the light-dependent reactions and Calvin cycle, but mitochondrial cellular respiration continued breaking down glucose and releasing $\text{CO}_2$, which formed carbonic acid and lowered the pH to $7.6$.
Monday, Nov 2 Short Answer (SA) Exam Blueprints: Dr. Irwin's Success Criteria Week 2 Summative SA Prep
✍️ How to Score 100% on Monday's Short Answer (SA) Section: Always name the exact organelle compartment (cytosol, mitochondrial matrix, cristae/inner membrane, intermembrane space, thylakoid membrane, thylakoid lumen, stroma), the exact electron carriers ($\text{NADH}/\text{FADH}_2$ vs. $\text{NADPH}$), and the direction of $\text{H}^+$ movement!
  1. Why Aerobic Respiration makes 36–38 ATP vs. Anaerobic 2 ATP (Role of $\text{O}_2$): Aerobic respiration uses Oxygen ($\text{O}_2$) as the final electron acceptor at the end of the ETC (forming $\text{H}_2\text{O}$). This allows the ETC to continuously accept electrons from $10\text{ NADH}$ and $2\text{ FADH}_2$, pumping $\text{H}^+$ into the intermembrane space to power ATP Synthase (oxidative phosphorylation = $32\text{–}34\text{ ATP}$) while regenerating $\text{NAD}^+$ and $\text{FAD}$ so Pyruvate Oxidation and the Krebs Cycle ($2\text{ ATP}$) can run. Without $\text{O}_2$, the ETC, Pyruvate Oxidation, and Krebs Cycle all stop; the cell can only run Glycolysis ($2\text{ net ATP}$ via substrate-level phosphorylation) paired with Fermentation (which makes $0\text{ ATP}$ and only regenerates $\text{NAD}^+$).
  2. Carbon, Electron, and Energy Flow in Respiration (From Dr. Irwin's Outline):
    • Carbon Flow: $\text{Glucose (6C)} \to 2\text{ Pyruvate (3C)} \to 2\text{ Acetyl-CoA (2C)} \to 6\text{ CO}_2\text{ (waste)}$.
    • Electron Flow: $\text{Glucose} \to \text{NADH \& FADH}_2 \to \text{ETC complexes in cristae} \to \text{O}_2\text{ (forming H}_2\text{O)}$.
    • Energy Flow: $\text{Glucose chemical bonds} \to \text{NADH/FADH}_2 \to \text{H}^+\text{ electrochemical gradient} \to \text{ATP}$.
  3. Why Leaves Turn Orange/Yellow in the Fall (Slide 13): Chlorophyll $a$ and $b$ absorb red and blue light and reflect green light. In the fall, cooler temperatures and shorter days cause plants to stop producing chlorophyll and existing chlorophyll breaks down, unmasking the already-present accessory pigments like carotenoids/carotene (orange) and xanthophylls (yellow).
