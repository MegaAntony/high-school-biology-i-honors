1. Enzymes (high priority)
  • An enzyme is a protein catalyst. It speeds up a reaction and is not consumed.
  • It lowers activation energy (Ea), the energy needed to start the reaction.
  • It does not change ΔG, the start or end energy levels, or the products formed.
  • Substrate binds the active site to form an enzyme-substrate complex. Enzymes are specific because of active-site shape.
  • Lock and key: site is rigid and matches. Induced fit: site adjusts around the substrate (the accepted model).
  • Shape comes from tertiary structure (H bonds, ionic bonds, disulfide bridges, hydrophobic interactions).
Trap: never say heat "killed" an enzyme. It is a molecule, not alive. Say denatured. Also, enzymes do not give a reaction energy. They lower the barrier.

4. ATP and ADP (high priority)
  • ATP = adenine + ribose + 3 phosphates. ADP has 2.
  • Hydrolysis: ATP + H₂O → ADP + Pi. Exergonic, releases usable energy.
  • Phosphorylation: ADP + Pi → ATP. Endergonic, energy comes from food (respiration) or light.
  • Coupling: ATP hydrolysis is paired with an endergonic reaction (muscle contraction, active transport, making molecules), usually by adding a phosphate to the target.
  • ATP is recycled constantly, not stored in large amounts.
Trap: the energy is not "in the phosphate" as a thing. It is released because the products (ADP + Pi) are more stable than ATP. Just know: breaking the terminal bond by hydrolysis releases energy.

  • ETC pumps H⁺ into the intermembrane space. H⁺ flows back through ATP synthase (chemiosmosis), which makes ATP.
  • O₂ is the final electron acceptor; it combines with electrons and H⁺ to make water.
  • Glycolysis does not need oxygen. Pyruvate oxidation, Krebs and ETC are the aerobic parts.
  • Cristae (folds) increase surface area for more ETC complexes and ATP synthase.
  • NADH and FADH₂ carry electrons to the ETC.
Trap: CO₂ is released in pyruvate oxidation and Krebs, not in the ETC. Water is made in the ETC.
6. Fermentation (medium)
  • Used when O₂ is absent. Glycolysis continues; ETC is stalled.
  • Its real job: regenerate NAD⁺ from NADH so glycolysis can keep running. It makes no extra ATP.
  • Lactic acid fermentation (muscles, some bacteria): pyruvate → lactate.
  • Alcoholic fermentation (yeast): pyruvate → ethanol + CO₂.
  • Fermentation gives about 2 ATP per glucose total vs far more with oxygen.

8. Photosynthesis (low)

6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + light → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂ · in chloroplasts

  • Light reactions: thylakoid membranes. Light + water → ATP, NADPH, and O₂ (from splitting water).
  • Calvin cycle: stroma. Uses ATP + NADPH to fix CO₂ into sugar (G3P).
  • Respiration and photosynthesis are linked: the products of one are the reactants of the other.
  • Mitochondria (respiration) and chloroplasts (photosynthesis) both make ATP using an H⁺ gradient and ATP synthase.
