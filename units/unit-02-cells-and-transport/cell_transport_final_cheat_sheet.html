🔢 1. The Numbers to Remember

  • 3 - 2 - 1 No-K: Sodium-Potassium pump exports 3 Na⁺ OUT, imports 2 K⁺ IN, and hydrolyzes 1 ATP.
  • 0.9% Saline: Normal vertebrate blood/body fluid is 0.9% salt (99.1% water). This is isotonic.
  • 0 ATP: All passive transport (Simple Diffusion, Facilitated Diffusion, Osmosis) uses ZERO energy and moves HIGH → LOW.
  • ATP Required: All active transport (Protein Pumps, Phagocytosis, Pinocytosis, Exocytosis, Endocytosis) requires ATP and moves LOW → HIGH (or bulk engulfment).

🧩 3. Membrane Proteins: TRACIE

  • T – Transport: Protein channels and carrier pumps that facilitate movement of polar/charged molecules across the bilayer.
  • R – Receptors: Signal transduction (e.g., peptide hormones bind surface receptors, triggering internal cellular responses).
  • A – Anchorage: Cytoskeleton attachment internally and extracellular matrix (ECM) anchoring externally for structural stability.
  • C – Cell Recognition: Glycoproteins and glycolipids serving as identification markers (e.g., ABO blood groups, immune self vs. non-self).
  • I – Intercellular Joinings: Tight junctions, desmosomes, and gap junctions that bind neighboring cells together.
  • E – Enzymatic Activity: Catalytic active sites localized on the membrane to accelerate sequential metabolic pathway steps.

🎯 4. Instant Scenario Matchers

  • White blood cell engulfing bacteria: Active Transport | Endocytosis (Phagocytosis)
  • Pancreas secreting insulin vesicles into bloodstream: Active Transport | Exocytosis
  • O₂ and CO₂ gas exchange across alveolar capillaries: Passive Transport | Simple Diffusion (0 ATP)
  • Glucose entering muscle cells via GLUT carrier: Passive Transport | Facilitated Diffusion
  • Rapid water flow into kidney/RBC via aquaporins: Passive Transport | Osmosis (Facilitated Diffusion of H₂O)
  • Freshwater Paramecium pumping out excess water: Active Transport | Contractile Vacuole (Uses ATP)
  • Gargling warm salt water for a sore throat / Slug salted: Osmosis | Hypertonic exterior draws water OUT
  • Eating salty chips makes you thirsty: Blood becomes hypertonic → Osmoreceptors trigger thirst & ADH

⚠️ 5. The 4 Biggest Teacher Traps to Avoid

1. "Permeable ONLY to X" Trap If a membrane is selectively permeable only to starch or only to water, other solutes (like glucose or salt) CANNOT move, even if they have a 90% concentration gradient! Only permeable substances move.
2. Plant Cells Bursting Trap Plant cells placed in pure distilled water NEVER burst. Their rigid cellulose cell wall creates opposing turgor pressure that halts net water influx. Animal cells burst (lysis); plant cells become turgid.
3. Dynamic Equilibrium Trap Molecules DO NOT stop moving when equilibrium is reached! Molecular motion is continuous and dynamic—particles cross in both directions at equal rates (Net flux = 0).
4. Dialysis Tubing & Macromolecule Trap In dialysis experiments: Iodine (Lugol's) is small and diffuses IN (turning starch purple/black). Starch is a large polymer and CANNOT cross the bag. Glucose is small and diffuses OUT (detected by Benedict's test).
