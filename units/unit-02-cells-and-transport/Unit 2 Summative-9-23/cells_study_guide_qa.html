Question 1

What is the function and location (plant, animal, bacteria) of all 22 organelles listed below?

Organelle / Structure Cell Location Biological Function
a. NucleusPlant & AnimalStores genetic code ($\text{DNA}$); regulates transcription, cellular reproduction, and metabolism.
b. ChloroplastsPlant (and algae)Site of photosynthesis: transforms sunlight, $\text{CO}_2$, and $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ into chemical glucose and $\text{O}_2$.
c. MitochondriaPlant & AnimalSite of cellular respiration: oxidizes pyruvate to generate cellular $\text{ATP}$ energy.
d. Rough ER (RER)Plant & AnimalMembrane studded with ribosomes; folds, modifies, and packages secretory proteins into vesicles.
d. Smooth ER (SER)Plant & AnimalSynthesizes lipids, detoxifies poisons/drugs, metabolizes carbs, and stores calcium ions.
e. CytoplasmAll (Plant, Animal, Bacteria)Semi-fluid cytosol suspending organelles; site of initial metabolic pathways.
f. RibosomesAll (Plant, Animal, Bacteria)Translates $\text{mRNA}$ into amino acid polypeptide chains (80S in eukaryotes, 70S in prokaryotes).
g. Cell MembraneAll (Plant, Animal, Bacteria)Selectively permeable bilayer maintaining intracellular homeostasis and controlling transport.
h. Golgi ApparatusPlant & AnimalReceives transport vesicles, modifies (glycosylation), sorts, and packages proteins for export.
i. LysosomesAnimal (Rare in plants)Acidic digestive vesicles containing hydrolytic enzymes; digests cellular waste and pathogens.
j. VacuolesPlant (Central) & Animal (Small)Storage of water, salts, and pigments; in plants, generates turgor pressure for rigidity.
k. CytoskeletonPlant & AnimalFiber network providing structural integrity, anchoring organelles, and facilitating motility.
l. CentriolesAnimal OnlyOrganizes mitotic spindle fibers during animal cell division; forms basal bodies of flagella.
m. NucleolusPlant & AnimalDense intra-nuclear region where $\text{rRNA}$ is transcribed and ribosome subunits are assembled.
n. Nuclear MembranePlant & AnimalDouble phospholipid bilayer separating the nuclear genome from cytoplasmic translation.
o. Nuclear PoresPlant & AnimalMultiprotein channels allowing $\text{mRNA}$ export and nuclear protein import.
p. Cell WallPlant (Cellulose) & BacteriaRigid extracellular matrix preventing osmotic rupture and maintaining cell architecture.
q. PlasmodesmataPlant OnlyMembrane-lined channels through plant cell walls for direct intercellular transport of fluids/ions.
r. Gap JunctionsAnimal OnlyConnexon protein rings connecting neighboring animal cells for rapid chemical/electrical signals.

Question 2

Who are the 5 cell scientists, and what did they do?

1. Robert Hooke (1665) Examined cork under early microscope; coined the term "cells" because the empty cellulose walls resembled monastery rooms.
2. Anton van Leeuwenhoek (1674) Grinded precision lenses; first to view living motile microorganisms in pond water, naming them "Animalcules".
3. Matthias Schleiden (1838) German botanist; concluded that all plants are composed of cellular structures.
4. Theodor Schwann (1839) German zoologist; concluded that all animals are composed of cellular structures.
5. Rudolf Virchow (1855) Proposed that all cells arise from pre-existing cells via cellular reproduction, disproving spontaneous generation.
Question 3

What are the 3 parts of the Cell Theory?

  1. All living organisms are composed of one or more cells.
  2. The cell is the most fundamental structural and functional unit of life.
  3. All cells arise from pre-existing cells through cellular division.
Question 4

What are the differences between plant and animal cells? (Venn Diagram)

Plant Cells Only:
  • Cellulose Cell Wall
  • Chloroplasts (Photosynthesis)
  • Large Central Vacuole
  • Plasmodesmata channels
  • Fixed rectangular geometry
Shared (All Eukaryotes):
  • Plasma Membrane
  • Nucleus & Nucleolus
  • Mitochondria
  • Rough & Smooth ER
  • Golgi Apparatus & Vesicles
  • Cytoskeleton & Cytoplasm
Animal Cells Only:
  • Centrioles / Centrosomes
  • Prominent Lysosomes
  • Small, multiple vacuoles
  • Tight & Gap Junctions
  • Irregular/Flexible geometry
Question 5

What are the differences between prokaryotes and eukaryotes? Give examples of each.

Prokaryotes:
  • No nucleus: DNA is in a naked nucleoid.
  • No membrane-bound organelles (no ER, Golgi, mitochondria).
  • Circular DNA plasmid rings.
  • 70S Ribosomes (smaller).
  • Size: Small (0.1–5.0 $\mu\text{m}$).
  • Examples: Escherichia coli, Streptococcus, Methanogens (Domain Bacteria & Archaea).
Eukaryotes:
  • True nucleus enclosed in a double membrane.
  • Membrane-bound organelles (mitochondria, ER, Golgi, vacuoles).
  • Linear chromosomes wrapped around histone proteins.
  • 80S Ribosomes (larger).
  • Size: Large (10–100 $\mu\text{m}$).
  • Examples: Animals, Plants, Fungi (Yeast, Mushrooms), Protists (Amoeba).
Questions 6 & 7

Protein Synthesis Organelles & The Endomembrane Secretory Relay

Organelles Involved: Nucleus, Nucleolus, Ribosomes, Rough ER, Transport Vesicles, Golgi Apparatus, Secretory Vesicles, and Plasma Membrane.

1.Nucleus: DNA gene transcribed into $\text{mRNA}$.
2.Nuclear Pores: $\text{mRNA}$ exits into cytosol.
3.Ribosome & RER: Ribosome binds $\text{mRNA}$ and docks onto Rough ER, translating protein into lumen.
4.RER Lumen: Chaperones fold polypeptide into native 3D conformation; initial glycosylation occurs.
5.Transport Vesicle: Buds off RER carrying protein along microtubules.
6.Golgi (Cis Face): Vesicle fuses with Golgi; protein is sorted, modified, and given address tags.
7.Secretory Vesicle (Trans Face): Pinches off trans face loaded with processed protein.
8.Plasma Membrane (Exocytosis): Vesicle fuses with cell membrane, expelling protein outward (e.g., insulin).
Question 8

Compare prokaryotes and viruses; why are viruses not considered living?

Bacteria (Prokaryotes) are living, self-sustaining unicellular organisms with metabolic enzymes, a cell membrane, and 70S ribosomes that reproduce via binary fission.

Viruses are acellular inert biological particles composed only of a protein capsid enclosing a viral genome ($\text{DNA}$ or $\text{RNA}$).

Why Viruses Are Non-Living:
  • Not made of cells (violates Cell Theory).
  • Zero independent metabolism (cannot generate $\text{ATP}$).
  • Cannot maintain internal homeostasis.
  • Cannot replicate independently (obligate intracellular parasite).

Question 10

Organelle Collaboration & "Cease to Collaborate" Failure Analysis (Short Answer Table)

Organelles / Structure Primary Functions How They Collaborate Consequence If They Cease to Collaborate
Mitochondria and Chloroplast Chloroplast makes glucose + $\text{O}_2$; Mitochondria oxidizes glucose + $\text{O}_2$ for $\text{ATP}$. Chloroplast products fuel mitochondrial respiration; mitochondrial $\text{CO}_2$ fuels photosynthesis. Plant depletes glucose, mitochondrial respiration stops, cellular $\text{ATP}$ crashes, active transport fails, cell starves.
Cell Wall and Central Vacuole Central vacuole exerts hydrostatic pressure; cell wall provides rigid counter-resistance. Vacuole swells with water against cell wall to create turgor pressure holding plant upright. Vacuole loses water in hypertonic fluid; membrane shrinks away from wall (plasmolysis), causing plant wilting.
Ribosome and Rough ER (RER) Ribosome translates $\text{mRNA}$; RER folds and processes polypeptides. Ribosomes dock onto RER translocons, injecting growing chains into lumen for chaperone folding. Secretory proteins fail to enter ER lumen, misfolding and aggregating in cytosol; triggers apoptosis via UPR stress.
Rough ER and Golgi Apparatus RER packages folded proteins into vesicles; Golgi sorts and attaches glycosylation tags. RER buds transport vesicles that travel along microtubules to fuse with cis-face of Golgi. Proteins accumulate inside RER causing toxic dilation; Golgi starves of cargo; cellular secretion ceases.
Golgi Vesicles and Vacuoles / Lysosomes Golgi tags hydrolytic enzymes; lysosomes degrade cellular waste in acidic lumen. Golgi tags acid hydrolases with mannose-6-phosphate and ships them in coated vesicles to activate lysosomes. Lysosomes lack hydrolytic enzymes; undigested cellular debris accumulates, causing cellular swelling (Tay-Sachs model).
