🔥 1. Organelle Collaboration & "Cease to Collaborate" Failure Analysis (Short Answer CER)
⚠️ Teacher Focus Alert: Dr. Irwin designed Page 3 of the review packet specifically around organelle collaboration pairs and predicting the cellular consequences if they cease to collaborate. This is the cornerstone of the exam's Short Answer Section.
1. Mitochondria & Chloroplast Collaboration Metabolic Coupling

Primary Functions:

  • Chloroplast: Uses solar energy to synthesize glucose and oxygen: $6\text{CO}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{light} \to \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\text{O}_2$.
  • Mitochondria: Performs aerobic respiration to generate ATP: $\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\text{O}_2 \to 6\text{CO}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O} + 36\text{–}38\text{ ATP}$.

How They Collaborate: Direct biochemical loop in autotrophic plant cells. The organic products of chloroplasts serve as fuel for mitochondria, while mitochondrial byproduct gases ($\text{CO}_2$) supply chloroplasts.

Consequence If They Cease to Collaborate: If chloroplasts are inhibited or fail, the plant depletes internal glucose reserves. Deprived of substrate, mitochondrial aerobic respiration stops, cellular ATP crashes, active transport fails, and the plant cell starves and dies.
2. Cell Wall & Central Vacuole Collaboration Turgor Pressure & Rigidity

Primary Functions:

  • Central Vacuole: Stores water and dissolved solutes; exerts outward hydrostatic pressure (turgor).
  • Cell Wall: Rigid cellulose framework providing mechanical resistance and structural shape.

How They Collaborate: As the vacuole fills with water in hypotonic conditions, it presses against the rigid cell wall. The cell wall pushes back with equal force, creating high turgor pressure that keeps the plant erect without lysing.

Consequence If They Cease to Collaborate: In a hypertonic environment, water exits the vacuole. Outward pressure drops, causing the plasma membrane to detach and shrink away from the cell wall (plasmolysis). The plant loses turgor and wilts.
3. Ribosome & Rough Endoplasmic Reticulum (RER) Protein Synthesis Docking

Primary Functions:

  • Ribosome: Catalyzes peptide bond formation between amino acids guided by mRNA.
  • Rough ER: Membranous folds providing chaperone proteins for folding and initial glycosylation.

How They Collaborate: Ribosomes translating proteins with a signal sequence dock onto translocon channels on the RER, threading the nascent polypeptide into the ER lumen.

Consequence If They Cease to Collaborate: Synthesized secretory proteins cannot enter the ER lumen. Polypeptides misfold or aggregate in the cytosol, halting membrane receptor production and triggering apoptosis via the Unfolded Protein Response (UPR).
4. Rough ER & Golgi Apparatus Collaboration Vesicular Trafficking

Primary Functions:

  • Rough ER: Assembles and packages folded proteins into transport vesicles.
  • Golgi Apparatus: Modifies, sorts, and packages proteins into secretory vesicles.

How They Collaborate: RER buds transport vesicles that travel along microtubules to fuse with the cis-face of the Golgi, delivering proteins for carbohydrate tagging and sorting.

Consequence If They Cease to Collaborate: Vesicular transport halts. Proteins accumulate inside the RER causing severe ER swelling. The Golgi is starved of cargo, cellular secretion shuts down, and newly synthesized enzymes cannot reach lysosomes.
5. Golgi Vesicles & Vacuoles / Lysosomes Collaboration Hydrolytic Delivery

Primary Functions:

  • Golgi Vesicles: Sorts acid hydrolases and packages them into clathrin-coated vesicles.
  • Lysosomes / Vacuoles: Acidic compartments ($\text{pH} \approx 4.5\text{–}5.0$) containing digestive enzymes.

How They Collaborate: The Golgi tags digestive enzymes with mannose-6-phosphate and ships them in vesicles that fuse with endosomes to form mature, functional lysosomes.

Consequence If They Cease to Collaborate: Lysosomes lack hydrolytic enzymes. Undigested cellular waste and lipids accumulate inside lysosomes, causing pathological enlargement and cell death (the biochemical cause of Tay-Sachs disease).
📦 2. The Endomembrane Protein Synthesis Relay (Step-by-Step)

Trace the path of an exported protein (e.g., insulin) from gene to secretion:

  1. Nucleus: DNA gene sequence is transcribed into messenger RNA (mRNA).
  2. Nuclear Pores: mRNA transcript exits the nucleus through nuclear pore complexes into the cytosol.
  3. Ribosome on Rough ER: Ribosome binds mRNA, initiates translation, and docks onto the Rough ER membrane.
  4. RER Lumen: Polypeptide threads into the lumen; folds into native 3D tertiary structure and receives initial oligosaccharides.
  5. Transport Vesicle: A membrane vesicle buds off the RER enclosing the protein.
  6. Golgi Cis-Face: Vesicle travels along microtubules and fuses with the cis-face of the Golgi apparatus.
  7. Golgi Cisternae: Protein is enzymatically modified (sugars added/trimmed) and given a chemical address tag.
  8. Golgi Trans-Face: Protein is sorted and packaged into a secretory vesicle that pinches off the trans-face.
  9. Plasma Membrane & Exocytosis: Secretory vesicle moves to the cell membrane, fuses with the phospholipid bilayer, and expels the protein outside the cell.

⚡ 4. Passive vs. Active Transport Summary

Passive Transport ($\text{0 ATP}$)

Direction: Down gradient ($\text{HIGH} \to \text{LOW}$).

  • Simple Diffusion: Small nonpolar molecules ($\text{O}_2, \text{CO}_2$) pass directly through bilayer.
  • Facilitated Diffusion: Polar/charged particles pass via channel proteins (aquaporins) or carrier proteins (GLUT).
  • Osmosis: Diffusion of free water molecules across a selectively permeable membrane.

Active Transport ($\text{ATP Required}$)

Direction: Against gradient ($\text{LOW} \to \text{HIGH}$).

  • Molecular Pumps ($\text{Na}^+/\text{K}^+$): Hydrolyzes $1\text{ ATP}$ to pump $3\text{ Na}^+\text{ OUT}$ and $2\text{ K}^+\text{ IN}$.
  • Endocytosis: Bulk import via vesicles (Phagocytosis = eating, Pinocytosis = drinking, Receptor-mediated).
  • Exocytosis: Bulk export via vesicle fusion with plasma membrane.
