🔑 Complete Answer Key & In-Depth Conceptual Explanations

Question Correct Option Primary Concept / Subtopic Deep Scientific Explanation
1 A Membrane Chemistry Phospholipids are amphipathic molecules. Their hydrophilic, polar phosphate heads face outward toward the aqueous extracellular and intracellular fluids, while their hydrophobic, nonpolar fatty acid tails face inward away from water to minimize thermodynamic free energy.
2 B Fluid Mosaic Model The plasma membrane is described as 'fluid' because phospholipids and unanchored proteins move laterally within each leaflet, and 'mosaic' because the bilayer is studded with an assortment of proteins, steroids, and carbohydrate chains.
3 C Membrane Fluidity Cholesterol acts as a temperature buffer: at high temperatures, its rigid steroid rings restrain phospholipid movement, preventing the membrane from becoming overly fluid; at low temperatures, it prevents tight packing of fatty acid tails, preventing freezing.
4 D Selective Permeability The hydrophobic interior of the phospholipid bilayer acts as a selective barrier. Small, nonpolar molecules ($O_2, CO_2, N_2$) diffuse directly across, whereas large polar molecules (glucose) and charged ions ($Na^+, K^+$) cannot cross without transport proteins.
5 A Cell Recognition Short branched carbohydrate chains (oligosaccharides) covalently linked to surface proteins or lipids face exclusively toward the extracellular space to act as identification tags for cell-cell recognition and immune system self-surveillance.
6 B TRACIE Anchorage Peripheral membrane proteins do not penetrate the hydrophobic core; they attach to the surface of the membrane or to integral proteins. On the cytoplasmic face, they anchor actin microfilaments of the cytoskeleton to preserve cell shape.
7 C Passive Energetics Passive transport (diffusion, osmosis, facilitated diffusion) requires no metabolic energy (0 ATP) because molecules move down their concentration gradient powered entirely by their own thermal kinetic energy until dynamic equilibrium is reached.
8 D Equilibrium Dynamics Dynamic equilibrium is a balanced steady state where molecules continue to cross the membrane in both directions, but because the rate of entry equals the rate of exit, the net concentration on either side remains completely constant.
9 A Diffusion Rates Fick's laws of diffusion establish that the rate of diffusion increases when temperature is high (more thermal kinetic motion), the concentration gradient is steep (greater driving force), and particle size/mass is small (lower drag).
10 B The Permeability Trap (Page 5) Because the problem explicitly specifies the membrane is permeable ONLY to starch, glucose cannot cross. Starch moves down its concentration gradient from high concentration outside (60%) into the cell (10%).
11 C Facilitated Diffusion Glucose is a large, uncharged polar molecule ($180\text{ g/mol}$) that cannot cross the hydrophobic core of the bilayer. It enters cells down its concentration gradient via facilitated diffusion through specific GLUT carrier proteins that alternate between two conformations.
12 D Transporters Channel proteins form continuous open hydrophilic tunnels that allow rapid transit of specific ions or water. Carrier proteins bind their specific substrate, which induces a conformational change that translocates the solute across the membrane.
13 A Osmosis Fundamentals Osmosis is specifically the passive movement of water molecules across a selectively permeable membrane from an area of higher free water concentration (lower solute concentration / higher water potential) to an area of lower free water concentration (higher solute concentration).
14 B Aquaporins While small amounts of water can slowly traverse the lipid bilayer due to membrane vibrations, specialized tetrameric channel proteins called aquaporins facilitate the transit of up to 3 billion water molecules per second per channel in single file.
15 C Tonicity Definitions Solution X has 5% solute (95% water) and Solution Y has 20% solute (80% water). Because Solution X has a lower solute concentration and a higher water concentration than Solution Y, Solution X is hypotonic relative to Solution Y.
16 D Animal Osmosis A red blood cell contains approximately 0.9% dissolved solutes (99.1% water). In pure distilled water (100% water), water rushes into the cell down its osmotic gradient. Lacking a rigid cell wall, the animal cell membrane stretches until it lyses (hemolysis).
17 A Plant Osmosis Plant cells are enclosed by a strong, non-living cell wall composed of cellulose microfibrils. In hypotonic environments, water enters the central vacuole, pushing the plasma membrane against the cell wall. The resulting wall pressure creates healthy structural turgor.
18 B Plasmolysis In a hypertonic saline environment (10% NaCl), the external water potential is far lower than the cell's cytoplasm. Water exits the central vacuole via osmosis, causing the protoplast to shrink and pull away from the rigid cell wall, leaving the cell plasmolyzed.
19 C Houseplant Fertilizer (Page 20) Heavily concentrated fertilizer makes the surrounding soil solution strongly hypertonic relative to the root cells. Water diffuses OUT of the root cells down its concentration gradient via osmosis, causing loss of turgor pressure (plasmolysis) and severe wilting.
20 D U-Tube Osmometer Side A has 10% sucrose (90% water); Side B has 40% sucrose (60% water). Because sucrose cannot cross the semipermeable barrier, water moves by osmosis from Side A into Side B. Consequently, Side A loses volume (falls to 7 cm) while Side B gains volume (rises to 13 cm).
21 A Dialysis Tubing Lab The synthetic dialysis pores are large enough to allow small water, glucose, and iodine molecules to pass, but too small for large, branched starch polysaccharides. Iodine enters the bag and turns starch blue-black; glucose leaves the bag into the beaker.
22 B Gel Deco Cube Lab Distilled water (0% solute) establishes the steepest hypotonic concentration gradient relative to the solute-rich gel cube, producing the greatest net influx of water molecules and the highest positive percentage change in mass (% ΔMass).
23 C Gel Lab Blotting Protocol Gently blotting the cube with a paper towel removes clinging surface liquid droplets that are not inside the cube, ensuring that the measured mass reflects only the water that actually crossed the membrane.
24 D Clinical Case Study CER Rapid ingestion of extreme volumes of water dilutes extracellular blood sodium (hyponatremia), making blood plasma hypotonic relative to intracellular fluids. Water moves into brain cells via osmosis; enclosed inside the rigid skull, swelling produces fatal cerebral edema.
25 A Clinical Brain Edema Treatment Administering hypertonic 3% saline raises the solute concentration of blood plasma above that of the swollen brain tissue. By osmosis, water diffuses out of the brain cells and into the blood vessels, reducing intracranial pressure.
26 B Freshwater Fish in Ocean The internal cells of a freshwater fish are hypotonic relative to hypertonic seawater. Water rapidly diffuses OUT of the fish's body by osmosis into the ocean, causing severe cellular dehydration and death.
27 C Active Transport Active transport moves chemical substances against their concentration gradient (from low to high concentration), a thermodynamically non-spontaneous process that requires coupling to metabolic energy (ATP hydrolysis).
28 D Sodium-Potassium Pump The Na+/K+ ATPase uses the energy from hydrolyzing 1 molecule of ATP to actively export 3 Na+ ions out of the cytoplasm and import 2 K+ ions into the cell, both moving against their respective concentration gradients.
29 A Membrane Potential By exporting 3 positive sodium charges while importing only 2 positive potassium charges per cycle, the pump creates a net loss of 1 positive charge from the inside, generating an electrical membrane potential (negative interior relative to exterior).
30 B Phagocytosis Phagocytosis is a specialized form of endocytosis ('cell eating') where the cell extends amoeboid pseudopodia around large particulate matter (such as whole bacteria), enclosing it in a membranous phagosome that fuses with an acidic lysosome.
31 C Pinocytosis While phagocytosis is targeted engulfment of large, visible solids (cellular debris or bacteria), pinocytosis ('cell drinking') is the continuous, non-specific invagination of the plasma membrane to sample surrounding extracellular fluid and dissolved solutes.
32 D Receptor-Mediated Endocytosis Receptor-mediated endocytosis is highly selective. Specific extracellular molecules bind to cell surface receptors clustered in membrane regions coated on their cytoplasmic side with the protein clathrin, triggering rapid endocytic vesicle budding.
33 A Exocytosis Secretion Secretory proteins (such as insulin) are synthesized by bound ribosomes on the Rough ER, packaged into transport vesicles that fuse with the cis face of the Golgi apparatus for sorting/modification, and bud from the trans Golgi as secretory vesicles that fuse with the plasma membrane.
34 B Throat Osmosis Inflamed mucosal tissues in a sore throat are swollen with excess interstitial fluid (edema). Warm salt water creates a temporary hypertonic bath that draws excess water out of the inflamed throat cells by osmosis, relieving tissue swelling and pain.
35 C Slug Desiccation A terrestrial slug possesses moist, mucus-covered external epithelial tissues. Pouring dry salt crystals over the slug creates an overwhelmingly hypertonic exterior environment, drawing internal body water out by osmosis and causing rapid desiccation and death.
36 D Contractile Vacuoles Freshwater protists like Paramecium are hypertonic to their pond water environment, causing water to continuously enter via osmosis. Because they lack a rigid plant cell wall, they utilize ATP-powered contractile vacuoles to collect and pump out excess incoming water.
37 A Strawberry Osmosis Adding granular sugar to strawberries creates a concentrated, hypertonic syrup layer on the fruit exterior. Water diffuses out of the strawberry cells down its concentration gradient via osmosis, creating the sweet syrup pool.
38 B Clinical IV Fluids Human blood cells are in dynamic osmotic equilibrium with normal physiological plasma (0.9% saline). Infusing pure distilled water would create a hypotonic bloodstream, causing red blood cells to absorb water, swell, and burst (fatal intravascular hemolysis).
39 C Cholera Toxin Scenario Flooding the intestinal lumen with chloride and sodium ions makes the digestive tract strongly hypertonic. Water rapidly leaves intestinal epithelial cells by osmosis to balance the gradient, resulting in severe watery diarrhea and acute systemic dehydration.
40 D Potato Lab Data Core 1 gained mass (+24%), proving it was in a hypotonic medium (0% sucrose). Core 2 experienced zero mass change (0%), demonstrating an isotonic equilibrium (1% sucrose). Core 3 lost mass (-22%), proving it was in a hypertonic medium (10% sucrose).
41 A Skin Wrinkling in Dishwater Epidermal skin cells contain dissolved solutes and proteins, making them hypertonic relative to the surrounding hypotonic dishwater. Water moves down its osmotic gradient into the cells, causing them to absorb water and swell into wrinkled ridges.
42 B Marine Fish in Freshwater Marine fish tissues are adapted to be roughly isotonic with salty ocean water. When placed into freshwater (0% salt), the surrounding water is strongly hypotonic. Water rushes into the fish's cells via osmosis, causing massive cellular swelling, lysis, and death.
43 C Penetrating vs Non-Penetrating Solutes Because sucrose cannot penetrate the membrane, it remains trapped in the extracellular space, maintaining a persistent osmotic gradient that continuously draws water out of the cell. In contrast, urea diffuses across the membrane until it reaches equilibrium, eliminating the osmotic gradient.
44 D Solute vs Solvent (Gimkit) A solution is a homogeneous mixture composed of one or more solutes dissolved within a solvent. The solute is the substance being dissolved (e.g., glucose, sodium ions), and the solvent is the liquid agent that dissolves the solute (water is the universal biological solvent).
45 A Dynamic Equilibrium in Saline In an isotonic environment, the system is in dynamic equilibrium. Water molecules continue to diffuse across the phospholipid bilayer and aquaporins in both directions, but because the rate of water influx equals the rate of water efflux, there is zero net volume change.
46 B Cystic Fibrosis CFTR Channel Normally, chloride ions are pumped out into airway secretions, creating an osmotic gradient that draws water out to thin the mucus. When CFTR channels fail, chloride and water remain trapped inside the epithelial cells, leaving the extracellular mucus dehydrated, thick, and sticky.
47 C Plant Cell Flaccidity in Isotonic Fluid Plant cells depend on high turgor pressure to maintain upright posture against gravity. In an isotonic solution, net water movement is zero; the central vacuole is not pressurized against the cell wall, leaving the cell limp or flaccid and causing the plant stem to wilt.
48 D Strawberries in Pure Water Pure distilled water (100% water) is hypotonic to the sugar-rich strawberry cells. Water moves by osmosis down its concentration gradient into the strawberry cells, swelling them until tissues become over-saturated and mushy.
49 A Airborne Simple Diffusion Aroma molecules are volatile gaseous particles that move randomly via Brownian motion. They spontaneously disperse from the region of highest concentration (the kitchen oven) to areas of lower concentration throughout the house via simple diffusion without any energy input.
50 B TRACIE Intercellular Joinings The 'I' in the TRACIE membrane mnemonic stands for Intercellular Joinings. Membrane proteins of adjacent cells hook together in various forms of junctions (tight junctions to prevent leakage, desmosomes to provide mechanical anchorage, and gap junctions for intercellular communication).
51 C Salty Snack & Thirst Osmoreceptors Ingested sodium is absorbed into the bloodstream, elevating extracellular solute concentration (hypertonic blood plasma). Water moves out of brain osmoreceptor cells in the hypothalamus via osmosis down its concentration gradient. The resulting cellular shrinkage fires neural signals that trigger the perception of thirst and release ADH to conserve water.
52 D Homeostasis Definition & Triad Homeostasis refers to an organism's ability to maintain relatively constant internal physiological conditions despite changes in the external environment. Three foundational biological examples are thermoregulation (sweating/shivering), blood glucose control (insulin/glucagon), and osmoregulation (kidney filtration and water balance).
53 A Percent Change in Mass Formula The standard biological formula for percent change in mass is: % ΔMass = [(Final Mass - Initial Mass) / Initial Mass] * 100%. Substituting the data: [(8.2 g - 5.0 g) / 5.0 g] * 100% = (3.2 / 5.0) * 100% = +64.0%.
54 B TRACIE Enzymatic Activity The 'E' in TRACIE represents Enzymatic Activity. Many membrane-bound proteins are enzymes whose catalytic active sites face either the extracellular or intracellular fluid (e.g., digestive enzymes like lactase on intestinal microvilli), allowing them to catalyze chemical reactions at the cell surface.
55 C Gummy Bear Gelatin Trapping A gummy bear is a gel made of cross-linked gelatin protein polymers packed with sugar solutes. The microscopic pores in the gelatin matrix are permeable to small water molecules but restrict the outward diffusion of trapped sugar molecules. Water enters by osmosis down its concentration gradient, swelling the gelatin matrix without losing sugar.
