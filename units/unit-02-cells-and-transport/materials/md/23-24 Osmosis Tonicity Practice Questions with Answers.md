## 📋 Overview & Study Strategies

When solving tonicity and osmosis problems:
1. **Identify Solute Concentrations:**
   - **Hypotonic:** Lower solute concentration (higher water concentration / higher water potential).
   - **Hypertonic:** Higher solute concentration (lower water concentration / lower water potential).
   - **Isotonic:** Equal solute concentration (dynamic equilibrium; no net movement).
2. **Determine Water Movement:**
   - Water **always** diffuses down its own concentration gradient from **high water concentration to low water concentration** (or from hypotonic solution toward hypertonic solution).
3. **Analyze Cell Responses:**
   - **Animal Cells:** Swell and may burst (**lysis/cytolysis**) in hypotonic solutions; shrink/shrivel (**crenation**) in hypertonic solutions; normal in isotonic solutions.
   - **Plant Cells:** Swell and become firm (**turgid**) in hypotonic solutions due to rigid cell walls and large central vacuole (normal/healthy state); become limp/flaccid in isotonic solutions; plasma membrane pulls away from cell wall (**plasmolysis**) in hypertonic solutions.

## ❓ Practice Questions & Detailed Solutions

### Question 1
> If you soak your hands in dishwater, you may notice that your skin absorbs water and swells into wrinkles. This is because your skin cells are _______________ to the _______________ dishwater.
> 
> - **A.** Hypotonic … hypertonic  
> - **B.** Hypertonic … hypotonic  
> - **C.** Hypotonic … hypotonic  
> - **D.** Isotonic … hypotonic  
> - **E.** Hypertonic … isotonic  

**Correct Answer:** **B. Hypertonic … hypotonic**

**Detailed Explanation:**
- Water moves into the skin cells causing them to absorb water and swell.
- By osmosis, water moves from an area of higher water concentration (hypotonic solution) into an area of lower water concentration / higher solute concentration (hypertonic solution).
- Therefore, the skin cells contain a higher solute concentration (**hypertonic**) relative to the surrounding bath/dishwater, which has a lower solute concentration (**hypotonic**).

### Question 2
> You decide to buy a new fish for your freshwater aquarium. When you introduce the fish into its new tank, the fish swells up and dies. You later learn that it was a fish from the ocean.  
> Based on what you know of tonicity, the most likely explanation is that the unfortunate fish went from a(n) _______________ solution into a(n) _______________ solution.
> 
> - **A.** isotonic, hypotonic  
> - **B.** hypertonic, isotonic  
> - **C.** hypotonic, hypertonic  
> - **D.** hypotonic, isotonic  
> - **E.** isotonic, hypertonic  

**Correct Answer:** **A. isotonic, hypotonic**

**Detailed Explanation:**
- Marine (ocean) fish have internal body fluids that are adapted to be approximately **isotonic** to their natural salty ocean environment.
- When placed into a freshwater aquarium, the surrounding water is **hypotonic** (virtually 0% salt) compared to the fish’s internal tissues.
- Water rapidly rushes into the fish’s cells by osmosis, causing severe swelling, cellular lysis, and death.

### Question 3
> In osmosis, water always moves toward the _______________ solution: that is, toward the solution with the _______________ solute concentration.
> 
> - **A.** isotonic, greater  
> - **B.** hypertonic, greater  
> - **C.** hypertonic, lesser  
> - **D.** hypotonic, greater  
> - **E.** hypotonic, lesser  

**Correct Answer:** **B. hypertonic, greater**

**Detailed Explanation:**
- Water moves from areas of high free water concentration (low solute) to low free water concentration (high solute).
- A **hypertonic** solution has a **greater** solute concentration. Hence, water always moves toward the hypertonic solution.

### Question 4
> The concentration of solutes in a red blood cell is about 2%. Sucrose cannot pass through the membrane, but water and urea can. Osmosis would cause red blood cells to shrink the most when immersed in which of the following solutions?
> 
> - **A.** a hypertonic sucrose solution  
> - **B.** a hypotonic sucrose solution  
> - **C.** a hypertonic urea solution  
> - **D.** a hypotonic urea solution  
> - **E.** pure water  

**Correct Answer:** **A. a hypertonic sucrose solution**

**Detailed Explanation:**
- For osmosis to drive net water out of a cell and cause shrinking, the external solution must contain a non-penetrating solute at a concentration higher than the cell interior (hypertonic).
- Since **sucrose cannot pass through the membrane**, it creates an effective osmotic gradient that pulls water out of the cell.
- In contrast, **urea can pass freely** through the membrane; urea molecules will equilibrate across the membrane, eliminating the concentration difference and preventing sustained osmotic water loss.

### Question 5
> Sea water is dangerous to drink because:
> 
> - **A.** one cup of sea water contains enough sodium to poison you.  
> - **B.** sea water is hypertonic to your body tissues and drinking it will cause you to lose water.  
> - **C.** sea water is isotonic to your body fluids and you will absorb too much water.  
> - **D.** the salt causes hypertension and you will promptly die of a stroke.  
> - **E.** it contains toxic levels of iodine.  

**Correct Answer:** **B. sea water is hypertonic to your body tissues and drinking it will cause you to lose water.**

**Detailed Explanation:**
- Seawater has a salt concentration of ~3.5%, whereas human body fluids and red blood cells are ~0.9% salt.
- Ingesting seawater introduces a highly **hypertonic** fluid into the gastrointestinal tract. Water is drawn out of body cells and blood plasma by osmosis into the gut, accelerating severe cellular dehydration.

### Question 6
> If the volume of a cell increases when it is placed in a solution, that solution is said to be _______________ to the cell.
> 
> - **A.** hypertonic  
> - **B.** subatomic  
> - **C.** isotonic  
> - **D.** gin and tonic  
> - **E.** hypotonic  

**Correct Answer:** **E. hypotonic**

**Detailed Explanation:**
- When a cell’s volume increases, net water is entering the cell.
- Water enters a cell when the outside environment has a lower solute concentration (higher water potential) than the cell interior, meaning the external solution is **hypotonic**.

### Question 7
> Inside one osmosis bag\*, A, is a 50% glucose solution and inside bag B is a 20% glucose solution. Both bags are put into beakers containing 100% water.  
> \*Osmosis bags are membranes that let water through but not glucose.
> 
> - **A.** Bag A will gain weight.  
> - **B.** Bag B will gain weight.  
> - **C.** Both bags will gain weight.  
> - **D.** Both bags will lose weight.  
> - **E.** Both bags will remain the same.  

**Correct Answer:** **C. Both bags will gain weight.**

**Detailed Explanation:**
- Beaker water is 100% water (0% glucose).
- Both Bag A (50% glucose / 50% water) and Bag B (20% glucose / 80% water) are hypertonic relative to pure water.
- Water moves from high water concentration (100% in the beaker) into both bags.
- Therefore, **both bags will gain weight** (Bag A will absorb water faster and gain more total mass due to a steeper concentration gradient, but both gain weight).

### Question 8
> A 0.9% NaCl solution is isotonic to red blood cells. Which of these describes the results if red blood cells are placed into a 9% solution of NaCl?
> 
> - **A.** They will burst.  
> - **B.** They will shrink.  
> - **C.** Nothing will happen.  
> - **D.** They will expand but not burst.  
> - **E.** None of the above.  

**Correct Answer:** **B. They will shrink.**

**Detailed Explanation:**
- 9% NaCl is ten times more concentrated than the isotonic 0.9% NaCl level, making it strongly **hypertonic**.
- Water rapidly leaves the red blood cells by osmosis to balance the external solute concentration.
- The red blood cells shrivel and shrink (crenation).

### Question 9
> Wallway is a new general herbicide for aquatic plants. Its main ingredient is a marine salt solution. It is effective against freshwater but not saltwater plants. It works by breaking down the cell walls of the plants. The freshwater plants die because their cells:
> 
> - **A.** swell and cease to function.  
> - **B.** shrink.  
> - **C.** remain the same size but malfunction.  
> - **D.** are crushed by the weight of the plant.  

**Correct Answer:** **B. shrink.**

**Detailed Explanation:**
- Marine salt solutions are hypertonic to freshwater plant cells.
- In a hypertonic environment, water exits plant cells through osmosis. The cytoplasm and central vacuole lose water, pulling away from the cell wall (**plasmolysis**), causing cells to **shrink** and desiccate.

### Question 10
> A red blood cell has a salt concentration of 0.9%. What will happen if it is placed into a 1% salt solution? The red blood cell will:
> 
> - **A.** shrink if its membrane is permeable to both the salt and the water.  
> - **B.** shrink if its membrane is impermeable to the salt and permeable to the water.  
> - **C.** maintain its shape - nothing will happen.  
> - **D.** swell and probably burst because its membrane is impermeable to salt and permeable to water.  
> - **E.** swell and probably burst because its membrane is impermeable to water and permeable to salt.  

**Correct Answer:** **B. shrink if its membrane is impermeable to the salt and permeable to the water.**

**Detailed Explanation:**
- The external solution (1.0% salt) has a higher solute concentration than the internal cell environment (0.9% salt), making the external solution slightly **hypertonic**.
- Because the plasma membrane is selectively permeable (impermeable to NaCl ions without active transporters, but freely permeable to water via aquaporins/diffusion), water moves out of the cell toward the higher solute concentration.
- As water leaves, the cell will **shrink**.

### Question 11
> Flasks X, Y, and Z contain solutions with different concentrations of the solute NaCl:
> - **Flask X:** 0.5% NaCl  
> - **Flask Y:** 0.9% NaCl  
> - **Flask Z:** 1.5% NaCl  
> 
> Red blood cells (0.9% NaCl) will be placed into each flask. Predict what will happen to the blood cells in each of the flasks:
> 
> - **A.** Flask X: Contract | Flask Y: Unchanged | Flask Z: Swell  
> - **B.** Flask X: Swell | Flask Y: Unchanged | Flask Z: Contract  
> - **C.** Flask X: Unchanged | Flask Y: Swell | Flask Z: Contract  
> - **D.** Flask X: Unchanged | Flask Y: Unchanged | Flask Z: Unchanged  

**Correct Answer:** **B. Flask X: Swell | Flask Y: Unchanged | Flask Z: Contract**

**Detailed Explanation:**
- **Flask X (0.5% NaCl):** Hypotonic to RBC (0.9%). Water moves *into* cells $ightarrow$ **Swell** (may lyse).
- **Flask Y (0.9% NaCl):** Isotonic to RBC (0.9%). Net water movement is zero (dynamic equilibrium) $ightarrow$ **Unchanged**.
- **Flask Z (1.5% NaCl):** Hypertonic to RBC (0.9%). Water moves *out of* cells $ightarrow$ **Contract** (shrink/crenate).
