Unit 2: Cellular Processes Extended Vocabulary Guide

Biology I Honors • South Forsyth High School (Dr. Irwin)

Source: Official Canvas Extended Academic Vocabulary

[!NOTE]
These terms are intended to be embedded in the curriculum and used with fluency to demonstrate mastery of cell structures and transport processes.

Term Official Definition
Active Transport The movement of substances across a cell membrane against their concentration gradient, requiring energy in the form of ATP and often facilitated by transport proteins.
Cell The structural and functional unit of life, capable of carrying out all processes necessary for survival. A plasma membrane encloses cells and may contain organelles.
Cell Membrane / Plasma Membrane A phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins that surrounds the cell, separating its internal environment from the external environment. It regulates the passage of substances into and out of the cell.
Cell Theory Cell theory is a fundamental concept in biology stating that all living organisms are composed of one or more cells, that cells are the basic units of structure and function in living organisms, and that all cells arise from pre-existing cells.
Cell Wall A rigid structure located outside the plasma membrane in plant cells, fungi, and some prokaryotes. It provides structural support, protection, and shape to the cell.
Cellulose A polysaccharide consisting of glucose monomers that forms the structural component of the cell walls in plants and some algae. It provides strength and rigidity to plant cells.
Centrioles Paired cylindrical structures composed of microtubules found in animal cells, involved in organizing the mitotic spindle during cell division.
Centrosomes Form the mitotic spindle in cells that lack centrioles.
Chitin A structural polysaccharide composed of modified glucose monomers, found in the exoskeletons of arthropods and the cell walls of fungi. It provides support and protection.
Chloroplasts Membrane-bound organelles found in plant and algal cells where photosynthesis occurs. They contain chlorophyll and other pigments that capture light energy and convert it into chemical energy.
Cholesterol Component of the fluid mosaic model that is responsible for helping maintain fluidity.
Concentration Gradient The difference in concentration of a substance between two regions, usually across a membrane.
Cytoplasm/ Cytosol The semifluid matrix within a cell that contains organelles, cytosol, and various dissolved molecules. It is the site of many cellular processes.
Cytoskeleton A network of protein filaments and tubules that provide structural support, maintain cell shape, facilitate cell movement, and transport materials within the cell.
Desmosomes Anchoring junctions between animal cells that provide mechanical strength and resistance to mechanical stress.
DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid) The molecule that stores genetic information in all living organisms, composed of two strands forming a double helix.
Endocytosis The process by which cells engulf large molecules or particles by forming a vesicle from the cell membrane.
Endomembrane System A network of membrane-bound organelles including the endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, lysosomes, and vesicles that work together to modify, package, and transport proteins and lipids within the cell.
Equilibrium A state of balance or stability in a system where opposing forces or influences are equal.
Eukaryote A type of cell that contains a membrane-bound nucleus and organelles. Eukaryotes include protists, fungi, plants, and animals.
Exocytosis The process by which cells expel large molecules or waste materials by fusing a vesicle containing the material with the cell membrane, releasing its contents outside the cell.
Facilitated Diffusion The passive transport of molecules across a cell membrane via transport proteins, moving down their concentration gradient.
Flaccid Describes a plant cell that is limp or wilted due to the loss of water in a hypertonic environment.
Fluid Mosaic Model A model describing the structure of the cell membrane as a fluid phospholipid bilayer embedded with various proteins that can move laterally within the membrane.
Gap Junctions Junctions between animal cells that allow direct exchange of ions and small molecules, facilitating communication and coordination.
Golgi Apparatus A stack of flattened membrane-bound sacs (cisternae) involved in modifying, sorting, and packaging proteins and lipids for secretion or delivery to other organelles.
Glycolipid This is a molecule that is part of the Fluid Mosaic Model that is part carbohydrate and part lipid. Glycolipids primarily contribute to the stability of the cell membrane and are key for cellular recognition
Glycoprotein This is a molecule that is part of the Fluid Mosaic Model that is part carbohydrate and part protein. Glycoproteins also play a role in immune responses and form the basis for receptors
Homeostasis The maintenance of stable internal conditions within an organism despite external changes. It involves regulation of variables such as temperature, pH, and ion concentrations.
Hypertonic Describes a solution with a higher solute concentration compared to another solution. Cells placed in a hypertonic solution may lose water and shrink.
Hypotonic Describes a solution with a lower solute concentration compared to another solution. Cells placed in a hypotonic solution may gain water and swell.
Integral Proteins Proteins that penetrate the lipid bilayer. Many of these proteins are used to transport larger molecules and ions across the membrane.
Intermediate Filaments Intermediate in size compared to microtubules and microfilaments, these protein fibers provide structural support and help anchor organelles in place.
Isotonic Describes two solutions with equal solute concentrations. Cells placed in an isotonic solution experience no net movement of water.
Ligand A molecule that binds to a specific receptor protein, initiating a cellular response.
Lysosomes Membrane-bound organelles containing digestive enzymes responsible for breaking down macromolecules, old organelles, and foreign substances within the cell.
Microfilaments Thin, solid protein fibers composed of actin protein subunits, involved in cell shape, movement, and cell division.
Microtubules Hollow cylindrical structures composed of tubulin protein subunits, involved in cell division, intracellular transport, and maintaining cell shape.
Mitochondria Double-membrane-bound organelles that are the primary sites of cellular respiration, where ATP (adenosine triphosphate) is generated from the breakdown of carbohydrates, fats, and proteins.
Multicellular Consisting of multiple cells organized into tissues, organs, and organ systems, each with specialized functions.
Nuclear Membrane/ Envelope A double membrane that surrounds the nucleus, separating its contents from the cytoplasm and regulating the passage of molecules between the nucleus and cytoplasm.
Nuclear Pores Openings in the nuclear membrane that allow the passage of molecules such as RNA and proteins between the nucleus and cytoplasm.
Nucleolus A dense region within the nucleus where ribosomal RNA (rRNA) is synthesized, and ribosomal subunits are assembled.
Nucleus A membrane-bound organelle that contains the cell's genetic material (DNA) and controls cellular activities by directing protein synthesis and cell division.
Organelle Specialized subunits within a cell that have specific functions. Examples include the nucleus, mitochondria, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, and lysosomes.
Osmosis The diffusion of water across a selectively permeable membrane from an area of higher water concentration to an area of lower water concentration.
Passive Transport The movement of substances across a cell membrane along their concentration gradient, without the input of energy.
Peptidoglycan A polymer consisting of sugar chains cross-linked by short peptides, found in the cell walls of bacteria. It provides structural support and protection.
Peripheral Protein A protein that is on the surface of the lipid bilayer and will not penetrate the lipid bilayer. The main functions of peripheral proteins include acting as enzymes and receptors, serving as structural anchors that connect the cell membrane to the cytoskeleton, and facilitating cell signaling and recognition.
Peroxisomes Membrane-bound organelles containing enzymes involved in the breakdown of fatty acids and the detoxification of harmful substances, particularly hydrogen peroxide.
Phagocytosis Type of endocytosis that the cell will bring in solid materials
Pinocytosis Type of endocytosis that the cell will bring in liquids.
Plasmodesmata Channels that connect adjacent plant cells, allowing for the exchange of water, nutrients, and signaling molecules.
Prokaryote A type of cell that lacks a membrane-bound nucleus and organelles. Prokaryotes include bacteria and archaea.
Protein Pumps Membrane proteins that actively transport molecules across a cell membrane by using energy from ATP.
Receptor A protein molecule located on the cell surface or within the cell that binds to specific signaling molecules, such as hormones or neurotransmitters, initiating a cellular response.
Receptor-Mediated Endocytosis A type of endocytosis that is signaled to begin when receptors fill with a certain chemical and bring it into the cell.
Response The specific cellular or physiological changes that occur as a result of receiving a signal.
Ribosomes Small, non-membrane-bound organelles composed of ribosomal RNA (rRNA) and protein. They are the sites of protein synthesis in the cell, where amino acids are assembled into polypeptides according to mRNA instructions.
Rough Endoplasmic Reticulum (RER) A network of membrane-bound tubules studded with ribosomes involved in protein synthesis, processing, and sorting.
RNA (Ribonucleic Acid) A single-stranded nucleic acid involved in protein synthesis and gene regulation, with types such as mRNA, tRNA, and rRNA.
Selective Permeability The property of a membrane that allows certain substances to pass through while preventing others from passing freely.
Semipermeable Cell Membrane The cell's outer layer that selectively allows certain substances to enter or exit, maintaining homeostasis.
Signal Transduction The process by which a signal (e.g., a hormone or neurotransmitter) is converted into a cellular response through a series of molecular events.
Simple Diffusion The spontaneous movement of molecules from an area of higher concentration to an area of lower concentration until equilibrium is reached, without the aid of transport proteins.
Smooth Endoplasmic Reticulum (SER) A network of membrane-bound tubules lacking ribosomes involved in lipid synthesis, detoxification, and calcium storage.
Solute A substance that is dissolved in a solvent to form a solution.
Solution A homogeneous mixture composed of a solvent and one or more solutes.
Solvent The liquid in which solutes are dissolved to form a solution.
Surface Area The total area of the cell membrane exposed to the external environment, important for nutrient absorption and waste removal.
Tight Junctions Specialized junctions between adjacent animal cells that form a continuous seal around the cell, preventing the leakage of fluids and molecules between cells.
Tonicity The relative concentration of solutes in a solution, which determines the direction of water movement by osmosis
Turgid Describes a plant cell that is swollen and firm due to the influx of water from a hypotonic environment.
Turgor Pressure The pressure exerted by the fluid (usually water) inside the central vacuole of a plant cell against the cell wall, providing structural support and rigidity to the cell.
Unicellular Consisting of a single cell capable of performing all necessary life functions.
Vacuoles Membrane-bound sacs within the cell that serve various functions, including storage of nutrients, waste products, and ions, as well as maintaining turgor pressure in plant cells.
Vesicle Small, membrane-bound sacs that transport materials within cells and play roles in metabolism and enzyme storage.
Volume The amount of space occupied by cellular contents, which influences metabolic rates and nutrient exchange.
