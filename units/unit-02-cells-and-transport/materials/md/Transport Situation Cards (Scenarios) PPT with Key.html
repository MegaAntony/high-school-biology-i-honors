Transport Situation Cards (Scenarios) with Key

Source: Transport Situation Cards (Scenarios) PPT with Key.pdf
Course: Biology I Honors — Unit 2: Cells and Cell Transport
Document Type: Practice Scenarios & Master Solution Key

📋 Task Overview & Instructions

For each scenario (#1–20):
1. Type of Transport: Identify whether the process is Passive (P) or Active (A).
2. Specific Form of Transport: Specify the precise mechanism:
- Simple Diffusion
- Facilitated Diffusion (channel or carrier protein)
- Osmosis
- Exocytosis
- Endocytosis (specifically Pinocytosis, Phagocytosis, or Receptor-Mediated Endocytosis)
- Molecular Protein Pump (e.g., Sodium-Potassium Pump)
3. Osmotic Analysis (if Osmosis applies):
- Identify the environmental solution type (Isotonic, Hypotonic, or Hypertonic).
- Specify the direction of net water movement (into the cell or out of the cell).
- State the cellular outcome (cell swells/bursts, cell shrinks/shrivels, or no net change).

🗂️ The 20 Scenarios: Detailed Analysis

Scenario 1

Situation: A tsunami causes sea salt water to wash into a garden bed and the plants wilt and die.
- Type of Transport: Passive (P)
- Specific Mechanism: Osmosis
- Environmental Solution: Hypertonic (saltwater has a much higher solute concentration than plant cell cytoplasm)
- Water Movement: Net water moves OUT of plant root and leaf cells
- Cellular Outcome: Plant cells lose internal turgor pressure; cytoplasm shrivels away from cell walls (plasmolysis), causing plants to wilt and die.

Scenario 2

Situation: The movement of $O_2$ molecules into and out of cells during respiration.
- Type of Transport: Passive (P)
- Specific Mechanism: Simple Diffusion
- Explanation: Oxygen is a small, nonpolar gas molecule that diffuses directly across the hydrophobic phospholipid bilayer down its concentration gradient (from high $O_2$ in arterial blood to low $O_2$ in metabolizing tissues).

Scenario 3

Situation: Low-density lipoproteins (LDL) outside of the cell bind to receptor proteins on the cell membrane. They are then taken into the cell through specialized coated pits that pinch off inside the cell.
- Type of Transport: Active (A)
- Specific Mechanism: Receptor-Mediated Endocytosis
- Explanation: Clathrin-coated pits recognize and bind specific LDL ligand particles, requiring cellular ATP to invaginate the membrane and form intracellular vesicles.

Scenario 4

Situation: Glucose molecules move across the membrane to supply the cell with energy. They must move through a carrier or channel protein embedded in the cell membrane because they are too large to go straight through the membrane.
- Type of Transport: Passive (P)
- Specific Mechanism: Facilitated Diffusion
- Explanation: Glucose is large and polar; it diffuses down its concentration gradient through specialized GLUT carrier/channel proteins without expenditure of ATP.

Scenario 5

Situation: Nerve cells secrete neurotransmitters in order to send signals to other neurons. (To secrete means to produce, release, or ooze).
- Type of Transport: Active (A)
- Specific Mechanism: Exocytosis
- Explanation: Synaptic vesicles filled with neurotransmitter molecules travel to the axon terminal, fuse with the presynaptic membrane, and dump their contents across the synaptic cleft via ATP-dependent vesicular fusion.

Scenario 6

Situation: What would happen if you took a sea turtle (ocean) and put it into Lake Lanier (freshwater)?
- Type of Transport: Passive (P)
- Specific Mechanism: Osmosis
- Environmental Solution: Hypotonic (Lake Lanier freshwater has nearly zero solute compared to the turtle’s marine-adapted internal fluids)
- Water Movement: Net water moves INTO the turtle’s cells
- Cellular Outcome: Cells take on excess water, swell, and undergo osmotic stress/lysis.

Scenario 7

Situation: Cells take in small amounts of solute and liquid particles by forming vesicles around them.
- Type of Transport: Active (A)
- Specific Mechanism: Endocytosis (Pinocytosis / "Cell Drinking")
- Explanation: Non-specific invagination of the plasma membrane engulfs droplets of extracellular fluid containing dissolved solutes into microvesicles using ATP.

Scenario 8

Situation: Plant cells secreting proteins and carbohydrates via vesicles to build cell walls.
- Type of Transport: Active (A)
- Specific Mechanism: Exocytosis
- Explanation: Golgi apparatus synthesizes and packages cellulose-synthesizing enzymes, hemicellulose, and pectin into vesicles that fuse with the cell membrane to export building materials outside the cell.

Scenario 9

Situation: 2 potassium ions ($K^+$) are moved into the cell while 3 sodium ions ($Na^+$) are moved out of the cell using a protein pump.
- Type of Transport: Active (A)
- Specific Mechanism: Sodium-Potassium Pump ($Na^+/K^+$ ATPase)
- Explanation: Primary active transport protein hydrolyzes ATP to move both ions against their respective steep concentration gradients (maintaining high external $Na^+$ and high internal $K^+$).

Scenario 10

Situation: The movement of $CO_2$ molecules into and out of cells during respiration.
- Type of Transport: Passive (P)
- Specific Mechanism: Simple Diffusion
- Explanation: Carbon dioxide is a small, uncharged nonpolar gas that diffuses freely through the phospholipid bilayer from high concentration inside respiring cells to lower concentration in surrounding blood capillaries.

Scenario 11

Situation: A foreign cell (like a bacterium) is engulfed by a white blood cell as an immune response. (To engulf means to surround or envelop).
- Type of Transport: Active (A)
- Specific Mechanism: Endocytosis (Phagocytosis / "Cell Eating")
- Explanation: White blood cells (e.g., macrophages) project actin-rich pseudopodia around the bacterial cell, enclosing it within a large phagosome for destruction by lysosomes.

Scenario 12

Situation: Someone sprays cologne in the corner of the room; eventually everyone in the room can smell it.
- Type of Transport: Passive (P)
- Specific Mechanism: Simple Diffusion
- Explanation: Scent molecules randomly disperse from an area of high concentration (spray nozzle) throughout the ambient air to areas of low concentration until evenly distributed.

Scenario 13

Situation: I put table sugar on strawberries and put them in the fridge overnight. When I looked the next morning, I saw that they were sitting in a pool of juice. What happened to the strawberries?
- Type of Transport: Passive (P)
- Specific Mechanism: Osmosis
- Environmental Solution: Hypertonic (sugar coating creates a very high solute / low water potential environment outside the strawberry cells)
- Water Movement: Net water moves OUT of strawberry cells
- Cellular Outcome: Strawberry cells lose water, shrivel (plasmolyze), and release intracellular juice into the bowl.

Scenario 14

Situation: The pancreas produces insulin (a hormone). This large molecule leaves the cell and enters the bloodstream via a vesicle.
- Type of Transport: Active (A)
- Specific Mechanism: Exocytosis
- Explanation: Pancreatic beta cells synthesize polypeptide insulin, package it in Golgi-derived secretory granules, and fuse with the plasma membrane using ATP to release insulin into adjacent blood capillaries.

Scenario 15

Situation: Someone bakes cookies and the smell travels throughout the house from the kitchen.
- Type of Transport: Passive (P)
- Specific Mechanism: Simple Diffusion
- Explanation: Volatile aromatic organic compounds disperse spontaneously through thermal kinetic motion from high concentration in the kitchen to low concentration in neighboring rooms.

Scenario 16

Situation: Why do you get thirsty after eating salty potato chips?
- Type of Transport: Passive (P)
- Specific Mechanism: Osmosis
- Environmental Solution: Hypertonic (ingested salt increases solute concentration in blood and extracellular fluids)
- Water Movement: Net water moves OUT of body cells into the hypertonic extracellular fluid
- Cellular Outcome: Tissue cells shrink; specialized hypothalamic osmoreceptor cells shrink and trigger neural thirst signals to restore fluid volume.

Scenario 17

Situation: The movement of solutes down ("with") the concentration gradient with the assistance of channel proteins.
- Type of Transport: Passive (P)
- Specific Mechanism: Facilitated Diffusion
- Explanation: Solutes flow through open pore-forming integral membrane channel proteins driven strictly by concentration gradients without ATP input.

Scenario 18

Situation: You leave a strawberry in a bowl of water. Eventually, the strawberry becomes plump and swollen. Overnight, the cell walls of the strawberry weaken, and the fruit becomes soft and mushy.
- Type of Transport: Passive (P)
- Specific Mechanism: Osmosis
- Environmental Solution: Hypotonic (pure bowl water has virtually zero solute relative to strawberry sugars/acids)
- Water Movement: Net water moves INTO strawberry cells
- Cellular Outcome: Cells absorb excessive water, swell to maximum turgor, and continuous osmotic stress degrades fruit cell walls making tissue mushy.

Scenario 19

Situation: Drinking salty seawater and then becoming very sick afterward.
- Type of Transport: Passive (P)
- Specific Mechanism: Osmosis
- Environmental Solution: Hypertonic (seawater is ~3.5% salt, far higher than human physiological 0.9%)
- Water Movement: Net water moves OUT of intestinal epithelial and body cells into the gut lumen
- Cellular Outcome: Severe systemic cellular dehydration, cell shrinkage, cramping, vomiting, and diarrhea.

Scenario 20

Situation: Snails can absorb water through their skin. What would happen if you poured salt onto a snail?
- Type of Transport: Passive (P)
- Specific Mechanism: Osmosis
- Environmental Solution: Hypertonic (solid salt grains create an extreme extracellular solute concentration on the snail’s moist, thin skin)
- Water Movement: Net water moves OUT of the snail’s body cells rapidly
- Cellular Outcome: Massive water loss causes snail cells to shrink and shrivel immediately, resulting in fatal desiccation.
