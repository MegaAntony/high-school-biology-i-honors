| **2** | **B** | Fluid Mosaic Model | The plasma membrane is described as 'fluid' because phospholipids and unanchored proteins move laterally within each leaflet, and 'mosaic' because the bilayer is studded with an assortment of proteins, steroids, and carbohydrate chains. |
| **3** | **C** | Membrane Fluidity | Cholesterol acts as a temperature buffer: at high temperatures, its rigid steroid rings restrain phospholipid movement, preventing the membrane from becoming overly fluid; at low temperatures, it prevents tight packing of fatty acid tails, preventing freezing. |
| **4** | **D** | Selective Permeability | The hydrophobic interior of the phospholipid bilayer acts as a selective barrier. Small, nonpolar molecules ($O_2, CO_2, N_2$) diffuse directly across, whereas large polar molecules (glucose) and charged ions ($Na^+, K^+$) cannot cross without transport proteins. |
| **5** | **A** | Cell Recognition | Short branched carbohydrate chains (oligosaccharides) covalently linked to surface proteins or lipids face exclusively toward the extracellular space to act as identification tags for cell-cell recognition and immune system self-surveillance. |
| **6** | **B** | TRACIE Anchorage | Peripheral membrane proteins do not penetrate the hydrophobic core; they attach to the surface of the membrane or to integral proteins. On the cytoplasmic face, they anchor actin microfilaments of the cytoskeleton to preserve cell shape. |
| **7** | **C** | Passive Energetics | Passive transport (diffusion, osmosis, facilitated diffusion) requires no metabolic energy (0 ATP) because molecules move down their concentration gradient powered entirely by their own thermal kinetic energy until dynamic equilibrium is reached. |
| **8** | **D** | Equilibrium Dynamics | Dynamic equilibrium is a balanced steady state where molecules continue to cross the membrane in both directions, but because the rate of entry equals the rate of exit, the net concentration on either side remains completely constant. |
| **9** | **A** | Diffusion Rates | Fick's laws of diffusion establish that the rate of diffusion increases when temperature is high (more thermal kinetic motion), the concentration gradient is steep (greater driving force), and particle size/mass is small (lower drag). |

| **11** | **C** | Facilitated Diffusion | Glucose is a large, uncharged polar molecule ($180\text{ g/mol}$) that cannot cross the hydrophobic core of the bilayer. It enters cells down its concentration gradient via facilitated diffusion through specific GLUT carrier proteins that alternate between two conformations. |
| **12** | **D** | Transporters | Channel proteins form continuous open hydrophilic tunnels that allow rapid transit of specific ions or water. Carrier proteins bind their specific substrate, which induces a conformational change that translocates the solute across the membrane. |
| **13** | **A** | Osmosis Fundamentals | Osmosis is specifically the passive movement of water molecules across a selectively permeable membrane from an area of higher free water concentration (lower solute concentration / higher water potential) to an area of lower free water concentration (higher solute concentration). |
| **14** | **B** | Aquaporins | While small amounts of water can slowly traverse the lipid bilayer due to membrane vibrations, specialized tetrameric channel proteins called aquaporins facilitate the transit of up to 3 billion water molecules per second per channel in single file. |
