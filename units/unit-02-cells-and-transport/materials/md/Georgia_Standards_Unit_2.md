## Unit 2: Cells and Cell Transport

* **Unit:** Unit 2 – Cells and Cell Transport
  * **Part 1:** Cell Membrane, Passive Transport, Active Transport, and Osmosis/Homeostasis

* **Weekly Focus (S1W5):**

  * Analyze osmotic environments (hypotonic, hypertonic, isotonic) and predict net water movement (*"Dilute the solute!"*).
  * Distinguish prokaryotic vs. eukaryotic cells, and plant vs. animal cells.
  * Relate organelle structures directly to their cellular physiological roles.

### **Specific Standard Elements**

#### **1. SB1.a — Cell Structures & Organelle Function (Unit 2, Part 2 Focus)**
* **Standard Description:**
  > *Construct an explanation of how cell structures, including the nucleus, cytoplasm, cell membrane, cell wall, chloroplasts, lysosomes, Golgi apparatus, endoplasmic reticulum, vacuoles, and mitochondria, facilitate physiological processes of life.*
* **Curriculum Alignment:**
  * **Cell Theory & Foundations:** All living organisms are made of cells, cells are the basic unit of life, and all cells arise from pre-existing cells.
  * **Prokaryotic vs. Eukaryotic Cells:**
    * *Prokaryotes:* Lack a membrane-bound nucleus and organelles; circular DNA in nucleoid; unicellular (e.g., bacteria).
    * *Eukaryotes:* Possess a true membrane-bound nucleus and specialized membrane-bound organelles; linear chromosomes; unicellular or multicellular (plants, animals, fungi, protists).
    * *Universal Cellular Features:* All cells contain DNA, ribosomes, cytoplasm, and a cell membrane.
  * **Plant Cells vs. Animal Cells:**
    * *Plant Cells:* Contain cellulose **cell walls**, photosynthetic **chloroplasts**, and a massive **large central vacuole** for turgor pressure.
    * *Animal Cells:* Contain **centrioles** (centrosomes for cell division) and abundant **lysosomes** for enzymatic waste breakdown; have smaller, multiple vacuoles.
  * **Organelle Specialization & System Interactions:**
    * **Nucleus:** Houses DNA and regulates gene expression and cell activities.
    * **Ribosomes:** Synthesize polypeptides and proteins from mRNA transcripts.
    * **Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER):** Rough ER (studded with ribosomes; folds and modifies proteins) and Smooth ER (lipid synthesis and detoxification); acts as intracellular highway.
    * **Golgi Apparatus:** Sorts, modifies, tags, and packages cellular products into vesicles for export or distribution.
    * **Mitochondria:** Sites of cellular respiration; oxidize glucose to synthesize ATP (cellular energy).
    * **Chloroplasts:** Sites of photosynthesis; capture radiant solar energy to synthesize glucose.
    * **Lysosomes:** Contain hydrolytic enzymes to digest metabolic wastes, pathogens, and worn-out organelles.
    * **Vacuoles:** Storage reservoirs for water, ions, nutrients, and cellular waste.
    * **Cytoplasm:** Semifluid cytosol matrix suspending organelles and enabling biochemical reactions.

#### **2. SB1.d — Cellular Transport & Homeostasis (Unit 2, Part 1 Focus)**
* **Standard Description:**
  > *Plan and carry out investigations to determine the role of cellular transport (e.g., active, passive, and osmosis) in maintaining homeostasis.*
* **Curriculum Alignment:**
  * **Cell Membrane Architecture (Fluid Mosaic Model):**
    * Phospholipid bilayer composed of hydrophilic (polar) phosphate heads facing aqueous environments and hydrophobic (nonpolar) fatty acid tails facing inwards.
    * Integral transport proteins (channel proteins, carrier proteins), peripheral proteins, cholesterol (fluidity buffer), and carbohydrates/glycoproteins (cell-cell recognition).
  * **Passive Transport (Down Concentration Gradient: High $\to$ Low; No Energy / ATP):**
    * **Simple Diffusion:** Direct passage of small, nonpolar molecules ($O_2, CO_2$) across the phospholipid bilayer.
    * **Facilitated Diffusion:** Movement of larger, polar, or charged particles (e.g., glucose, ions) through specific transmembrane protein channels.
    * **Osmosis:** Net diffusion of water molecules across a selectively permeable membrane towards regions of higher solute concentration (*"Water follows solute"* / *"Dilute the solute"*).
  * **Tonicity & Osmotic Environments:**
    * **Hypotonic Environment:** External solute concentration is lower than internal solute concentration $\to$ water enters the cell $\to$ cell swells (turgid in plant cells; risk of lysis in animal cells).
    * **Hypertonic Environment:** External solute concentration is higher than internal solute concentration $\to$ water exits the cell $\to$ cell shrinks/shrivels (crenation in animal cells; plasmolysis in plant cells).
    * **Isotonic Environment:** Solute concentrations are equal inside and out $\to$ water enters and leaves at identical rates (**dynamic equilibrium**) $\to$ constant cell volume.
  * **Active Transport (Against Concentration Gradient: Low $\to$ High; Requires Energy / ATP):**
    * **Molecular Pumps:** Carrier proteins that consume ATP to pump ions against concentration gradients (e.g., $Na^+ / K^+$ ATPase pump).
    * **Bulk Transport:**
      * **Endocytosis:** Vesicular engulfment of external particles, large solutes, or fluids into the cell (phagocytosis = cell eating, pinocytosis = cell drinking).
      * **Exocytosis:** Fusion of intracellular vesicles with the plasma membrane to secrete bulk materials, hormones, or wastes out of the cell.
  * **Homeostasis:** The active maintenance of internal equilibrium despite changing extracellular environments.
