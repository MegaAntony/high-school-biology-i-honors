### **1. Characteristics of Life & Scientific Method**

* **Cellular Organization:** All living things are composed of one or more cells (unicellular or multicellular).
* **Metabolism:** The sum of all chemical reactions occurring within an organism to obtain and use energy.
* **Homeostasis:** The process of maintaining a stable internal environment despite external changes (e.g., body temperature, pH).
* **Evolution & Adaptation:** The change in genetic traits and characteristics of a population over generations.
* **Qualitative Data:** Descriptive data based on observable characteristics and qualities (e.g., color, texture).
* **Quantitative Data:** Numerical data obtained through measurements and counting (e.g., mass, time, temperature).
* **Independent Variable (IV):** The variable intentionally manipulated/changed by the experimenter (plotted on the **x-axis**).
* **Dependent Variable (DV):** The variable measured or observed; responds to changes in the independent variable (plotted on the **y-axis**).
* **Control Group:** The benchmark group that does not receive the experimental treatment, used for comparison.
* **Experimental Group:** The group that receives the variable being tested.
* **Constants:** All conditions and factors that are kept strictly identical across all groups in an experiment.

### **2. Properties of Water**

* **Polarity:** A property of water where oxygen has a partial negative charge ($\delta^-$) and hydrogens have partial positive charges ($\delta^+$) due to unequal electron sharing.
* **Hydrogen Bond:** A weak electrostatic attraction between the partially negative oxygen of one water molecule and the partially positive hydrogen of an adjacent water molecule.
* **Cohesion:** The tendency of water molecules to stick to other water molecules (creates high surface tension).
* **Adhesion:** The attraction between water molecules and other polar or charged surfaces (e.g., water climbing plant xylem).
* **Capillary Action:** The movement of water upward through narrow spaces against gravity, resulting from the combination of cohesion and adhesion.
* **Surface Tension:** The measure of how difficult it is to stretch or break the surface of a liquid; created by cohesive hydrogen bonds at the air-water interface.
* **High Specific Heat:** Water requires a large amount of heat energy to raise its temperature by $1^\circ\text{C}$, buffering against extreme temperature changes in organisms and environments.
* **Density of Water (Solid vs. Liquid):** Ice is **less dense** than liquid water because hydrogen bonds lock into a spacious crystal lattice, allowing ice to float and insulate aquatic life below.
* **Universal Solvent:** Water dissolves a vast range of polar and ionic solutes due to its polarity, forming hydration shells around ions and polar molecules.
* **High Heat of Vaporization:** Water absorbs significant heat before evaporating, providing evaporative cooling (e.g., sweating).
* **Hydrophilic:** "Water-loving"; polar or ionic substances that dissolve or interact readily with water (e.g., sugars, salts).
* **Hydrophobic:** "Water-fearing"; nonpolar substances that repel and do not dissolve in water (e.g., lipids, oils).

### **3. Chemical Reactions & Organic Chemistry**

* **Organic Compound:** Carbon-based molecules containing carbon covalently bonded to hydrogen (and often O, N, P, S).
* **Inorganic Compound:** Compounds that lack carbon-hydrogen bonds (e.g., $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, $\text{CO}_2$, $\text{NaCl}$).
* **Monomer:** A small repeating chemical sub-unit (building block) that can chemically bond to form a polymer.
* **Polymer:** A large macromolecule composed of many covalently linked monomers.
* **Anabolic Reaction:** A metabolic pathway that synthesizes complex molecules from simpler ones, requiring energy input (e.g., building glycogen).
* **Catabolic Reaction:** A metabolic pathway that breaks down complex molecules into simpler units, releasing energy (e.g., digestion, cellular respiration).
* **Dehydration Synthesis (Condensation):** A chemical reaction that joins monomers into polymers by removing a water molecule ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$) as a byproduct (requires energy/anabolic).
* **Hydrolysis:** A chemical reaction that breaks polymers down into monomers by adding a water molecule ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$) across the bond (catabolic).

### **4. Macromolecules (The 4 Biomolecules)**

#### **A. Carbohydrates**
* **Elements:** Carbon, Hydrogen, Oxygen ($\text{C}, \text{H}, \text{O}$) in a typical $1:2:1$ ratio $(\text{CH}_2\text{O})_n$.
* **Monomer:** **Monosaccharide** (simple ring sugars like glucose, fructose, galactose; suffix *-ose*).
* **Dimer:** **Disaccharide** (two monosaccharides joined by a glycosidic bond; e.g., sucrose, lactose, maltose).
* **Polymer:** **Polysaccharide** (long chains of linked monosaccharides).
* **Key Polysaccharides:**
  * **Starch:** Energy storage in plants.
  * **Glycogen:** Short-term energy storage in animal liver and muscle tissue.
  * **Cellulose:** Structural support in plant cell walls (indigestible fiber for humans).
  * **Chitin:** Structural component of fungal cell walls and arthropod exoskeletons.

---

#### **B. Lipids**
* **Elements:** Carbon, Hydrogen, Oxygen ($\text{C}, \text{H}, \text{O}$ — high ratio of $\text{C}$ and $\text{H}$, very little $\text{O}$).
* **Key Characteristic:** Nonpolar and hydrophobic (insoluble in water).
* **Triglyceride Structure:** One glycerol molecule bonded to three fatty acid chains via ester linkages.
* **Saturated Fatty Acid:** Hydrocarbon chain with **only single carbon-carbon bonds**; saturated with hydrogens. Solid at room temperature ("bad fats", e.g., butter, lard).
* **Unsaturated Fatty Acid:** Contains **one or more double carbon-carbon bonds** ($\text{C}=\text{C}$), creating kinks/bends in the chain. Liquid at room temperature (oils).
* **Phospholipid:** Molecule with a glycerol backbone, two hydrophobic fatty acid tails, and a hydrophilic phosphate head group; forms the cell membrane bilayer.
* **Steroids:** Lipids characterized by a four-fused-ring carbon skeleton (e.g., cholesterol, estrogen, testosterone).

---

#### **C. Proteins**
* **Elements:** Carbon, Hydrogen, Oxygen, Nitrogen, Sulfur ($\text{C}, \text{H}, \text{O}, \text{N}, \text{S}$).
* **Monomer:** **Amino Acid** (20 standard types).
  * **Amino Acid Anatomy:** Central carbon bonded to a Hydrogen atom, an **Amino group** ($-\text{NH}_2$ / N-terminus), a **Carboxyl group** ($-\text{COOH}$ / C-terminus), and a variable **R-group** (side chain).
* **Bond:** **Peptide Bond** (covalent bond formed between the carboxyl carbon of one amino acid and the amino nitrogen of another via dehydration synthesis).
* **Polymer:** **Polypeptide** chain.
* **Levels of Protein Structure:**
  1. **Primary ($1^\circ$):** The linear sequence of amino acids linked by peptide bonds.
  2. **Secondary ($2^\circ$):** Local folding patterns (Alpha Helices and Beta Pleated Sheets) stabilized by hydrogen bonds along the polypeptide backbone.
  3. **Tertiary ($3^\circ$):** The 3D globular shape formed by interactions between R-groups (hydrophobic interactions, hydrogen bonds, ionic bonds, disulfide bridges).
  4. **Quaternary ($4^\circ$):** Association of two or more polypeptide subunits into a functional multi-protein complex (e.g., hemoglobin).
* **Denaturation:** Loss of a protein’s native three-dimensional conformation due to extreme heat or pH changes, causing loss of biological function.

---

#### **D. Nucleic Acids**
* **Elements:** Carbon, Hydrogen, Oxygen, Nitrogen, Phosphorus ($\text{C}, \text{H}, \text{O}, \text{N}, \text{P}$).
* **Monomer:** **Nucleotide** (composed of a 5-carbon pentose sugar, a phosphate group, and a nitrogenous base).
* **Polymers:** 
  * **DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid):** Double-stranded helical molecule containing deoxyribose sugar and bases A, T, C, G; stores hereditary genetic blueprints.
  * **RNA (Ribonucleic Acid):** Single-stranded molecule containing ribose sugar and bases A, U, C, G; assists in protein synthesis and gene expression.
* **Bond:** **Phosphodiester linkage** between the sugar of one nucleotide and the phosphate of the next (sugar-phosphate backbone).
